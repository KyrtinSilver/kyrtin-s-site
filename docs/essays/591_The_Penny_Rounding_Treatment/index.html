The “Penny Rounding” Treatment

591October 23, 2026

The “Penny Rounding” scheme of shaving the rounded pennies off of every transaction was famously entrenched in pop culture by Superman III (1983), which was later re-iterated in Office Space (but with incompetence in implementation), further raising the level of popular recognition. Someone or some group who adds little or no value to a process engages in a middle-man attack, scraping money from all transactions within their reach.

The irony is that few people seem to consciously recognize that this scheme is implemented globally, and at Office Space levels of incompetence, across numerous industries today. This “Penny Rounding” treatment also applies not only to monetary transactions, but perhaps more importantly to the time, cognitive capacities, integrity, and security of nearly every globalized process that one could name today.

Rather than only being a means of theft, to take something that is desired (absent right or merit), this treatment can also take the form of offloading something that is undesirable (absent justification or logical reason). For example, many of the “Bullshit Jobs” that David Graeber’s research covered exist partly or fully because something undesirable has been offloaded to a group who has no reason to carry that burden.

A common pattern of this takes the form of one government department or corporate division chasing after some KPI or arbitrary goal, blind to the consequences of their own actions, wherein any consequences are then pushed onto another department or division, often in additional to many or all of their citizens, customers, and/or clients. Many modern “immigration” processes have followed this pattern, where multiple departments and divisions each throw their own piles of horse shit on top of any adjacent departments and divisions, all of it piling up with little or no recourse for anyone buried under all of that shit.

However, government and corporate employees do get paid to perform those Bullshit Jobs, giving them a limited umbrella of mitigation. The people with no mitigation, and usually no substantive recourse to speak of, are everyone else, and everyone else also generally gets hit with those same piles of shit.

Let’s put this into some napkin math to make things more quantifiable, even if only in the ballpark. For a seemingly mundane example, take the premise that some new government form is required, with the following attributes of direct and predictable consequence:

  1. 1 hour of wasted time (per citizen)

  2. $25 of added (wasted) cost to every citizen, and $5 in processing cost per person to the government

  3. A 10% chance of further time and money being wasted (uncertainty)

  4. A 1% chance of extremely and/or uniquely bad outcomes (per citizen, uncertainty), markedly degrading the cognitive capacities of 20% of those filling out the forms for the processing period.

  5. A 15% chance of a major Cybersecurity breach of that government agency and their collected data (per year)

  6. A virtually 100% chance of degrading the integrity of the government in question, as virtually every additional government form with the above attributes serves to highlight the incompetence and/or malevolence of said government.

How often does policy advice consider such a list of consequences, even when they are all both direct and predictable? This isn’t even mentioning the 2nd and 3rd order consequences (some of which remain largely predictable), which in many cases exceed the severity of those noted above.

For that matter, when is the last time you saw someone or some group actually taking responsibility for such a list of consequences? Such decisions are made and policies implemented every day, and yet nobody takes responsibility for the consequences, no matter how direct and/or predictable they may be.

What would such responsibility even look like? Most people have never seen responsibility for large-scale actions being taken, unless it was an action dominantly perceived as positive. Let’s revisit the previous example, and say that a board of 5 directors decided to implement that new form, resulting in them being responsible for the following damage, a carbon copy of which they in turn deserve (according to Ethics):

  1. 1 hour of “mild” suffering per citizen whose time they waste (cumulative), divided by 5 Directors, or among those who voted in favor.

  2. $25 of debt, to be paid back to every citizen impacted, along with $5 in costs to be paid back to the government

  3. The reimbursement of all further time and money wasted being repaid, along with uncertainty imposed on said Directors as to when their debts will be repaid in full.

  4. The uncertainty as to if some extreme or uniquely bad outcome will land on each Director, from their perspective, along with the certainty of each director repaying in full each event where those outcomes were actualized among those impacted by their decisions.

  5. The repayment of all measurable damages resulting from data exposed by Cybersecurity breaches, along with the permanent public exposure of all such data belonging to said Directors.

  6. The damage to the integrity of an entire government is far more diffuse and intangible, making this vastly more difficult to package in a form for the repayment of this kind of debt, but a meaningful start is to reduce to integrity of the offending Directors to zero, preventing them from being allowed to make any such decisions moving forward.

Obviously, government employees don’t face such a list of consequences today, no matter how clearly they may deserve them. If they did, they’d probably spend the rest of their lives screaming in pain, all of their assets liquidated to go toward monetary debts that they could never realistically repay, to match their debts to the suffering of citizenry that they also could never realistically repay, short of virtually indefinite life extension technology.

The actions of such blatant criminals are disregarded as a matter of course, treated as “normal”, where the pointless and often severe suffering of entire populations become no more than a rounding error in the eyes of those inflicting growing and cancerous sources of societal damage.

Part of this traces back to matters of Cognitive Bias as well, shifting the “Overton Window” of which forms of government or corporately-originated Sadism are deemed as socially acceptable at a given moment. Suffering inflicted within the current window is treated as though it doesn’t exist, in an attempt to avoid retribution for those crimes. Likewise, (justified) retroactive corrections are systematically avoided.

Many also passively (and naively) assume that such suffering just “Magically Vanishes” over time, through the psychological equivalent of a delete button. However, physics and biology strongly, and demonstrably, disagree with this belief.

Everything that happens leaves a mark and has some impact. Even if that mark is to cause some damage that is repaired within a few minutes, resources are expended, focus diverted, and consequences exacted within the window of that damage being inflicted and repaired. This is a matter of both scale and chains of events, with biochemical chains, for example, being highly predictable.

Even if 99% of all consequences are overlooked or neglected, they still exist, and their impact remains. Those impacts in turn are generally cumulative, with only a truly tiny portion ever likely to directly cancel out one another. This is because virtually all such impacts take on high dimensionality forms of consequence, even when Cognitive Bias attempts to simplify them into linear spectrums.

The world is thus filled with “Penny Rounding” schemes, across many domains and scales, and the cumulative consequences of all such schemes attempting to maximize their own resource extraction within countless “Tragedies of the Commons”.

Another very common trick is used to turn such schemes into a “Business Model” today, where some small amount of actual value is offered, but the resources extracted in exchange for that small mote of actual value are inflated by an order of magnitude. This bears a strong resemblance to the manipulation method where a seed of truth is encapsulated within a thick shell of absolute bullshit, as has become common practice within many political arenas.

Examples of this malevolent business model include Amazon, AirBnB, Steam, Google Play, Apple’s App store, and numerous other common services. A common practice is for them to charge 30% of all revenue, such as the Google and Apple App stores and Steam, even though no more than a 3% fee could realistically be justified based on the actual value provided.

The rest is purely malicious exploitation, with any actual value further degraded by the presence of many different dark patterns, such as “sponsored” apps littering recommendations and searches. Besides some VERY limited quality assurance, Google Play and Steam are both just search and recommendation engines, with both of those functions being severely degraded in actual value by the vermin-ware of “sponsored” trash apps. Apple goes the opposite direction, making it so tedious to keep apps up to their standards that many won’t waste the time that they demand, a demand which itself should sharply decrease any cut that they could take from revenue.

Others like Amazon or AirBnB apply a variable fee, where Amazon’s can range from an only slightly overpriced 6% to a staggeringly malicious 45% “referral fee” on the total sales price. Amazon’s search functionality and recommendation engines are both infamously mediocre, making the actual value that they offer limited to mediocre aggregation and some (variable) ability to claw back refunds on defective products (which almost any platform has, as often legally required), both things of relatively low value. AirBnB is measurably less malicious, only reaching between 15.5 to 3+16.5% depending on the model selected.

It is important to note that the malevolence or lack thereof in pricing is relative to the value offered by said entity or platform, not the cost to said entity or platform by which it is delivered. Thus, it is technically possible to have a 90% profit margin while still delivering a level of value capable of justifying that markup, although such value would itself need to be extreme and/or unique to measure up.

I once pondered if the “Brain Fog” (mild brain damage) of COVID might prove cumulative, steadily degrading human cognition at a global scale with each passing season. Even if human biology adapts or adapted to prevent that outcome, the same thing has been steadily occurring and repeating via this “Penny Rounding” treatment across domains globally, where countless and growing sources of cognitive decline accumulate atop virtually every individual.

That currently alien concept of “taking responsibility for one’s own actions” can and realistically must be applied to mitigate the myriad horrors of the present day. Superman isn’t going to swoop in to save society from this scheme, but anyone can choose to become a hero, taking responsibility for their own actions, and making those actions distinctly positive, such as through dismantling the negative examples noted above. That responsibility applies to Ethics, not whatever arbitrary “laws” happen to prevail at a given place and moment in time, with the former marking how history is likely to remember such individuals.
