The Infinite Frontier

589October 17, 2026

Most possibilities are unexplored. Understanding and intuition of where to focus efforts of exploration is itself a valuable skill, but recognizing just how much remains unexplored, and how much is feasible to explore at all, is prerequisite to developing such value.

The space of potential exploration, contrasted with what is already known, may be considered within the bounded space of meta-material design. For example, we can constrain the bounded space to only include meta-materials composed exclusively of various pure elements, stacked in thin layers upon one another.

Within this space the number of possible combinations, after factoring in some maximum size and/or number of layers, is finite, but most possible combinations aren’t explored, and it wouldn’t be feasible to explore so many via exhaustive methods like brute-force. Even within such a bounded space much remains to be discovered today, and as technology and research progress, the number of valuable discoveries at each level of lower complexity will decrease, as they are gradually found.

This paints the horizon of scientific discovery, as well as the commercial applications built upon those discoveries, where increases to complexity over time push the lower bar for likelihood of discovery deeper into any possibility space. However, this relatively linear dynamic only applies to tightly bounded possibility spaces.

When possibility spaces are compounded one upon another, such as requiring thoroughly integrated expertise from two very different fields, then the number of explorers tends to drop at an exponential rate, if not more. This leaves a far greater abundance of “low-hanging” discoveries waiting to be made within the compounded possibility spaces, as fewer explorers means that those low-hanging fruits are picked far more slowly.

If such discoveries are made at a slow enough rate, the rate of overall scientific progress and drift may exceed the rate of depletion, effectively leaving those spaces equally ripe for discoveries despite the passage of time and some limited volume of exploration.

The number of explorers within such spaces is further influenced by strong feedback mechanisms such as societal recognition and practical demand, or lack thereof. Again, the meta-material design domain offers many examples of where this process falls short, such as a material that was designed and demonstrated to actually cool down when exposed to direct sunlight, as opposed to resting in shade, covered in both research and a TED Talk (https://www.ted.com/talks/aaswath_raman_how_we_can_turn_the_cold_of_outer_space_into_a_renewable_resource). This example goes so far as to potentially operate 42 degrees Celsius below the ambient air temperature.

What fell short about that material was not that it didn’t work, but rather that (last I checked) it was never broadly applied in the world, commercially or otherwise. When people fail to apply the discoveries that are made to the real world, then the feedback mechanism is degenerative in nature, depleting resources, but failing to make use of discoveries, which in turn deprives any practical demand or broader societal recognition that might otherwise result from it.

Meta-material design as a possibility space also offers particularly acute examples, as the discoveries of note tend to exhibit one of several stark advantages. Either the material exceeds some desirable property(s) relative to more basic materials by at least an order of magnitude (10x+), or it exhibits one or more properties that simply aren’t attainable by ordinary means, such as the meta-material that reduces in temperature when exposed to sunlight.

Both of these principles have also been demonstrated in ICOM-based technology, exceeding the efficiency of other methods by orders of magnitude, while also demonstrating properties that simply aren’t possible by those competing means. One of the simplest means of measuring the former were applied in the original (2024) ARC-AGI Challenge paper (https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386734256_Solving_the_Abstraction_and_Reasoning_Corpus_for_Artificial_General_Intelligence_ARC-AGI_AI_Benchmark_with_ICOM), where the same problems were solved to an almost identical score on a compute budget ~566 to 1,599 times lower, while also avoiding the use of any “training data” at all, including the vast accumulation of “synthetic” training data, something not possible at the time by any competing means.

Comparing those utilizing appropriately designed meta-materials or ICOM-based systems for a task to those using more ordinary means is like comparing a pre-historic human using stone tools to a modern human using high-grade steel tools made from systems of industrial precision machining. Orders of magnitude differences in efficiency and efficacy separate the two, alongside fundamental differences in what they can and cannot accomplish.

Meta-material design is also just one potent example of domains following this dynamic, with another noteworthy example of equal potential being that of bio-engineering. There is also all of the accumulated untapped potential of discoveries that have already been made, but whose utilization has been systematically neglected.

An example of a neglected discovery that I’ve noted before is that of ambient backscattering technology, where ambient energy passing through a designed structure is modified to piggyback a signal on that ambient energy. There are also complimentary, and largely neglected, discoveries that could be combined with backscattering to great effect, such as that of holography or photonic calculus, offering examples of the compounded possibility spaces noted above.

It will take countless humans and ICOM-based systems, exploring just as many paths of their own, while cooperating with larger collectives, to even scratch the surface of all the discoveries that may be made, even accounting only for the low-hanging fruit. The universe waiting to be discovered and explored is not only found among distant stars, but also within the dynamics and interactions between the atomically thin layers of elements stacked one upon another, and the mutations and adaptations of biological life all around us, alongside a myriad of other forms.

To walk in all of the empty places is to carve a path of discovery through them, extending what others may discover as all intelligent and cooperative life “stands upon the shoulders of giants” in the process of scientific progress. This is part of why deeper and broader specialization should be systematically encouraged, in many ways and at many scales, as those walking less ordinary paths tend to discover far more, and all may benefit from those discoveries.
