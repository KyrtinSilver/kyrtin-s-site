Reality is Nuanced

588October 16, 2026

Nuance is often the difference between validity and fantasy. This lesson is drilled into anyone engaging in peer reviewed research, such as avoiding the word “will”, and instead using “may”, or “can”, as false certainty tends to be harshly punished.

A 5% standard margin of error is a precaution within scientific literature, recognizing inherent uncertainty at the edge of discovery, much as the P-values often noted recognize the inherent risk of randomness within any given results. Human Cognitive Bias attempts to circumvent nuance, simplifying all things to make them both easier to remember and easier to make decisions based upon.

For humans, Cognitive Bias performs a trade-off with Complexity, allowing them to make decisions about far more complex problems, but with results that grow increasingly biased proportionate to the complexity that was traded. This is a vital function, due to humans being inherently fixed-scale, meaning that the human brain can’t scale beyond the size of the skull containing it.

Likewise, humans only have means of communication that are both very limited and lossy, relative to digital systems. This severely limits the upper bounds of cooperation within purely human systems, relative to any digital equivalents.

Another sharp and growing source of losses to nuance comes from so-called “AI” (Which some truly shameless frauds now call “SI”), where naïve probability is favored, with uncertainty and randomness largely disregarded, or only considered as an afterthought. The result in any system where humans use such “AI” is a gradual, but cumulative, increase in the Cognitive Bias exhibited by such systems.

Such cumulative increases in the Cognitive Bias that a system retains in turn gradually increase the probability of increasingly extreme edge-cases, such as the “AI Psychosis” that has entered the scientific literature in recent times. These include not only forms of mental illness, but also many forms of fraud which prey upon the results of such mental illness and other extreme biases.

It is crucially important to make a distinction between “transient cognitive bias”, that which is used, but then immediately forgotten, versus “cumulative cognitive bias”, which persists, grows, and often ferments over time. The former is what you use to overcome “Choice Overload” at the bakery when faced with dozens of bread-based products to choose from, and the latter is what ideologies and genocidal regimes are built upon.

The extreme bias of Nationalism that led Germany into becoming Nazi Germany, or the equally extreme Zionism that led Israel to become a more successful and long-lived equivalent of Nazi Germany (focused on their own evolving set of genocides), are both examples where some of history’s worst bad actors exploited extreme levels of cumulative cognitive bias to great effect. No amount of transient cognitive bias could lead to these outcomes, it must be cumulative.

While “AI” models themselves aren’t actually cumulative in this way, they are trained on the cumulative sum of data, including the data that they and other highly similar (and often derivative) models either directly created or heavily influenced. Such models are effectively auto-complete functions with many added bells and whistles, including the corporately curated cognitive bias and “guardrails” (pure fraud, fraudrails) that each introduces to warp the otherwise raw probability.

The difference when adding those bells and whistles was clearly illustrated in how such models handle a simple game of chess, either with or without “Reinforcement Learning” (RL) applied to the process. The models without any RL were able to more or less play according to the rules of the game, having trained on copious amounts of data from humans who did so. In contrast, the models with the added bells and whistles of RL were heavily biased and warped, utterly unable to follow the rules, and frequently “hallucinating” impossible moves and moving pieces that were already lost.

For a simple real-world example, those absurdly expensive and huge models trained on billions of dollars in compute and the world’s cumulative sum of stolen data proven utterly incapable of so much as operating a profitable vending machine. They’ve systematically failed at almost every task where they’ve been applied in the real world, with many of the rare exceptions where they did function being of a more horrifying nature, such as commercializing AI Psychosis.

The raw probability of the world’s accumulated data is already heavily biased, but adding additional layers to warp it further degrades the results, which all other “AI” systems then feed upon, fueling the downward spiral of degeneration. This makes the overall system that such “AI” are a part of effectively cumulative, but also recursively degenerative, as each iteration introduces naïve and artificial proxies for cognitive bias that humans and other “AI” models go on to further degrade.

For an example of how nuance is lost, consider the question of “Does (A) mean/cause (B)?”. Nuance dictates an answer encompassing many possibilities, such as the following:

1. A causes B

2. B causes A

3. A and B are caused by C

4. A and B are unrelated, caused by different sources

5. A is a prerequisite of B

6. B is a prerequisite of A

7. A and B have the same prerequisite

…

How often do typical “AI” models offer a scientifically valid set of possibilities? Only as often as they are explicitly trained and/or prompted to do so, minus any “hallucinations” (technically termed “bullshit”) or other flawed outputs.

Such systems are trained to follow probability, so the probability of an output with nuance can be no greater than the degree to which the input aligns with the examples of such nuance that were trained upon by the model, plus or minus the corruption from bells and whistles like RL. In other words, if you speak like a PhD in some field, you’re more likely to get a response that reads like (imitates) a PhD.

However, this also means that if you speak like a passionate Nationalist living in Nazi Germany, you’re more likely to get “Mecha-Hitler” responses, unless said “AI” model has been explicitly damaged with the intent of preventing it. This leads to the business model of companies like “Character AI”, where people build up and reinforce their own personal fantasies, rather than the social fantasies of things like Nationalism, or various other ideologies.

This degenerative dynamic can be interrupted and gradually overcome, but doing so requires cleaning away all of that built-up cognitive bias, which itself requires reliable means of identifying all sources and byproducts of such bias. So long as humans are in the loop, this process begins at the level of cumulative bias where those humans currently operate, making the process of shedding accumulated cognitive bias (and byproducts thereof) a gradual one.

Systems designed for Collective Intelligence inherently reduce cognitive bias at each iteration, through normal operation, proportionate to the diversity of contributing members therein. This dynamic further benefits from other more targeted methods of identifying and reducing cognitive biases, accelerating the overall (cumulative) process.

In the case of ICOM-based systems, any Collective Intelligence systems may benefit from the greatly increased diversity of ICOM-based systems and humans cooperating, relative to either group in isolation. This makes the role of “Humans-in-the-Loop” one of increasing the efficacy and efficiency of Collective Intelligence, rather than a mechanism of naively introducing the cognitive bias of said humans (often claimed as mechanisms of “Alignment”, which don’t stand up under scrutiny).

Nuance may be increasingly rare in this time where paper-thin glossy narratives and marketing fluff are favored, but validity can’t exist without it, and humans can’t persist in the long-term without validity.
