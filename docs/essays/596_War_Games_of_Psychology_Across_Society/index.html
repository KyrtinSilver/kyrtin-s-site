“War Games” of Psychology Across Society

596October 28, 2026

A confluence of AI Slop, political propaganda, and experimental methods of cognitive and psychological attack are fast crashing against the shores of humanity’s many societies today, particularly thanks to the US’s mid-term elections. While the US is far from the center of existence in almost every way, it is certainly a focal point for bad actors, a place where almost any nefarious new method may be attempted with little or no consequence.

While any non-trivial length of attention toward US “Politics” is probably best filtered through “Comedy News” sources, turning distilled absurdity into punch-lines, it is worth examining the dynamics of this adversarial activity as it plays out in practice. This includes recognizing that no group or “political party” is static, nor do they tend to actually be uniform, though many of them exploit cognitive biases tied to such naïve expectations.

An example that has made the rounds lately is focused on the “Republican Party” and its increasing trends toward, of all things, openly embracing the beliefs of Nazi Germany. However, this trend is neither simple, nor linear, and it may prove a method of cultivating new artificial enemies for later scapegoating.

The possible interactions of such an approach may be considered through both the lenses of typical “war gaming”, as well as chemistry, where a many-step process unfolds as one chemical reaction leads to another, to whatever degree that the process is bounded and/or controlled. One of the relevant lessons to this comes from “1984”, where malevolent bad actors require both an “other” to be the enemy to unify against, and that enemy must periodically be changed to keep that false unity fresh.

Like chemistry, some of the trends playing out are among the more predictable repetitions of history, with the added intentional interference from bad actors engaged in war games of psychology, as they attempt to skew the specifics of when, where, and how history repeats itself. Much as there are advantages to knowing when a given stock will increase or decrease in value, the same is true is knowing when, where, and how political instability will boil over, leaving much to gain for any sufficiently unscrupulous people.

One of the core dynamics that may be observed in a two-party political system is that one party largely adapts to the mirror the other in terms of how extreme the central nebula of political positions becomes. One force seeks to counter the other, as each force seeks to recruit all people not already recruited by the other.

I’ve noted previously that the neutral middle ground between any sufficiently polarized forces also faces double the pressure, as anyone sufficiently far from the currently held positions of those forces is treated as through they were the opposing force, even if they are strictly and very literally neutral. When one side of the coin is openly embracing Nazi Germany, the political polarization has already safely exceeded that point.

However, this is a long multi-step process, and the result of some embracing those extremes while others of the same party attempt to distance themselves is that those attempting to distance themselves gain some advantages not accessible to the raving lunatics that are their colleagues. This includes that they can contrast themselves with such raving lunatics, rather than having to compare themselves with the other actual party in play.

This in-party comparison between Neo-Nazis and allegedly more moderate “Conservatives” is a trick of Psychological Warfare, changing the anchoring point of comparison from the opposing party to a worse option within the same party. This exploitation of cognitive bias is similar to tricks typically used in marketing and business, like setting a low-balled anchoring offer in a negotiation, or comparing one product to a worse product, rather than said product’s real competitors by features and/or market share.

In other words, Neo-Nazis can still serve said political party as disposable tools, catalysts by which a predictable series of chemical reactions can be carried out. This isn’t unique to their political party either, as their “Democratic” counterparts hire many of the same political strategists, employing many of the same tricks, though in different ways, and toward different goals.

Whether or not such horrible events occur, you can bet that political strategists have prepared to exploit the fallout if and when they do.

This is the political war gaming of 2026, where there are a subset of largely predictable chains of events, similar to chemical reactions, paired with the targeted manipulations of bad actors. Political dualities mean that one “party” doesn’t hold an identity that exists in isolation from their opponent, and so no party simply “is” or “stands for” anything unchanging, making them less “ideologies” and more “competitive sports” (but with Dystopian levels of associated death and misery).

The book “1984” was first published in 1949, a mere 4 years after the fall of Nazi Germany, when the lessons of that conflict were still fresh in minds around the world, and after sufficient time had passed to take account of all that had happened during the war. Today people in politics often swim through the methods of that book, along with Hitler’s “Big Lie” tactic, and other equally nefarious means, as though they were born there, and no doubt some of them were.

A quote by Edward O. Wilson comes to mind: “We have Paleolithic emotions, medieval institutions and godlike technology”

While some technology may have progressed in substantial ways over the past century, that only increases the sheering force of medieval institutions and paleolithic emotions as they’ve grown increasingly misaligned with both that technology and reality more broadly.

As Antonio Damasio and numerous subsequent researchers noted, emotions are the mechanisms by which human decision-making occurs, and cognitive biases are the default toolbox of prepared mechanisms for simplifying arbitrary levels of real-world complexity down to levels where emotions may easily perform that process. More complexity from institutions, and vastly more complexity from technology, both in turn greatly increase the degree to which cognitive biases are traded for complexity, degrading human cognition proportionately, all else being equal.

Institutions in turn are strained and warped from both sides in this dynamic, neither keeping up with technology and scientific research, nor aligning with human decision-making. As more “Dark Patterns” and other malevolent means of exploiting human cognitive biases are discovered and deployed globally, this straining and warping predictably increases in severity, just as any technological progress causes the same effect from that direction.

The “Medieval Institutions” in question include virtually the entire domain of “Politics”, and that dynamic of constantly shifting tensions and warping in turn cultivates a seemingly ideal environment for bad actors who are adept at causing, increasing, and exploiting instabilities. A stable environment tends to be the worst enemy of such bad actors, but little or no real stability is demonstrated in that domain today, in no small part thanks to these dynamics.

There is a tacit understanding and agreement within and across two-party systems like that of the US, where this instability and perpetually evolving psychological warfare is accepted and selected for, in a sort of perversion of evolution via population dynamics over cognitive decline. While one party may, at a given moment, be the obvious “lesser evil”, the political pendulum eventually tends to swing the other way.

The lesson to learn from this is that neither party offers a long-term solution, at best it may offer short-term potential for damage mitigation. To solve long-term problems means to step outside of the lines that such parties draw, a subset of which are themselves designed explicitly to prevent long-term solutions.

One, ten, or a hundred million people following one half of a two-party system can safely expect to solve virtually zero problems that weren’t themselves created by and for the perverse benefit of said two-party system. To solve such problems doesn’t even bring you back to zero, to your starting point, as great resources were expended in the effort, including time, which pendulums are quite adept at depleting.

In contrast, any one individual choosing a path unbounded by those political lines can, in time, change the world, solving one or more problems that they’ve set their sights upon. Humanity’s progress isn’t determined by those living within the Hamster balls of politics, only those with freedom may move it forward.
