Slopularity

605November 8, 2026

While AI Slop is no longer “new”, it is still steadily contaminating the groundwater of the internet. One of the effects of this lead-poisoning-esc kind of brain-rot has found fertile ground in the waste-waters of YouTube, taking an increasing variety of distinct forms.

The horrors of AI-slop videos full of gore and animated cats gained media attention previously, videos which parents should lose custody of their children over (and be prevented from making any more children following). However, the vast majority of AI-slop is both less overtly horrifying, and more subtle in less emotionally provocative ways. While the infamous criminals behind such gore videos often followed an ideological narrative, the majority of actual AI Slop instead follows a path of least resistance in more economic terms, particularly where parasitic activity is optimized to feast on the lazy.

One example of that majority is the rapid rise of AI Slop music, where videos rake in millions of views by generating popularly searched genres of music (or arbitrary combinations thereof). This follows the logic of only needing the highest probability of being clicked on within a ranked (search) list, where the listener isn’t given reason to leave the page for long enough that their attention drifts elsewhere while it plays.

Within this slop there are no real “songs”, but rather you get hour-long imitations of a genre of music, where the variation over time hovers only slightly above raw repetition. This is another instance of cognitive biases in action, promoting laziness (“cognitive frugality”), where the music never quite becomes bad enough to justify attention being returned to the selection of something better, where a Sloplist (AI-Slop Playlist) of music might keep running all day.

If the religious concepts of purgatory had a soundtrack, it would sound like such “music”.

The cognitive impact of this is more subtle in terms of potency per time interval, but when that negative impact is near-constant, the net result will tend to be greater than short instances of more overtly horrifying AI Slop exposure. For most people and in most cases, there are distinct benefits to switching gears, where songs end and someone looks up from their digital screens like a groundhog at the end of winter.

The videos attached to such AI Slop also tend to be so easy to spot that I’ve begun to recognize their movement patterns within peripheral vision alone, not needing to so much as focus my eyes on them. They use short and often looped clips, frequently of AI-generated women dancing or playing instruments, generic B-roll type backgrounds, and/or the more clown-like animated horrors.

As is commonly found among parasites and “invasive species” within nature, the real-world effect of this Slop is to starve out the actual musicians whose music was stolen and used to train those AI models. As the Slopularized music is itself optimized to be generic genre-based background music, the loss of actual musicians turns this into a degenerative cycle, where the Overton Window steadily shifts downward, leading the Slopularized music that people select to become progressively worse.

Much like YouTube’s recommender algorithms infamously cultivated a pedophilia ring where a new account clicking on a single such video then entered a perpetual loop with nothing but more of the same, it is a safe bet that clicking on any instances of such AI Slop may cause similar persistent detriments. For this reason I’ll abstain from linking any such videos directly.

Music is the “canary in the coal mine” for a much broader set of problems in this case, as many domains may be similarly drowned in Slop, where strong financial incentives encourage all technically capable bad actors to do so. What makes music a more appealing target for bad actors to steamroll first is that it is heavily bounded, with a finite selection of genres, instruments, notes, flourishes, and so on, while also having a large audience and rapid high-volume feedback mechanisms for optimization and economic exploitation.

This allows for many potential problems in music generation to be quickly resolved by deterministic means and rapid high volumes of feedback, while remaining at relatively low levels of complexity. One step up the ladder of complexity takes you from the levels of such Slopularized music to a place where “search results”, “news”, and “advertising” exist, a space where bad actors can now apply many of the same lessons they learned at lower levels of complexity.

It isn’t just scientific progress that learns and advances based upon “standing on the shoulders of giants”, bad actors also advance their own malevolent means by “standing on the shoulders of Eldritch horrors”, like the Shoggoth of LLM-based technology. The success of each individual bad actor within these Tsunamis of Slop in turn feed into the more global machinations of criminals like Jensen Huang and the AI Bubble more broadly, as more extreme damages accumulate across numerous industries, further amplified by artificially-induced geopolitical tensions.

If your friends and family start routinely sending you Slopularized content, be it music, “news”, “ads”, or whatever worse incarnations of the same that may emerge from here on out, I strongly recommend holding a funeral for them. They’re already dead in the cognitive sense, and the noises they make thereafter may as well be attributed to the gaseous chemical exchanges of a corpse as it decays. Remember them as they were in life, and move on.
