Infinite Combinations across Iterative Exploration

585October 13, 2026

As mentioned previously, the diversity that contributes to systems of Collective Intelligence may be considered as “paths”, and a path may exist for an individual, a subset of an individual’s life, or at the meta-path of an organization, where many paths converge to create something not possible from any one isolated source. Each path offers a unique value and perspective, which may in turn be combined.

This combinatorial process can exist at many different scales and across several different dimensions. For example, the different paths that an individual walks, like their life in school, professional career, family life, and so on, all contribute to the overall path that said person walks, which in a dominantly deterministic universe is both who they are, as well as who they become.

The process takes place both within the individual, as well as between that individual and their peers, and so on at each increasing scale across society, where many paths come together to contribute to something larger than themselves. However, when systems of Collective Intelligence are absent then nothing guarantees that the convergence of such paths aren’t destructive, potentially making the sum less than the value of the contributing parts in those cases.

A different kind of meta-path can also exist in a sort of “Lineage”, where rather that operating in parallel, the influence is inherited from previous iterations over time. However, this form of meta-path only maintains stability and suitability within domains that themselves retain much of the same contextual grounding over time.

Each path may be visualized fairly easily within a graph structure, where different clusters of elements are taken as context for consideration at each iterative step of the journey. This is mirrored in the structure of ICOM-based systems, as applied to the Global Workspace, where a subset of all knowledge and experience available to a system is selected as the subset that is relevant to guide a given process or subject that the system has focused its attention on.

At each step of this journey, whether considered in visualization across a graph structure or within ICOM-based systems specifically, there is also the counterfactual shadow of all of the context that wasn’t selected, all of the values and connections that weren’t held for the selected subset, and all of the paths of the selected subset that weren’t explored. Each represents adjacent, but unexplored, branches leading off of that journey’s path.

There is also every other path currently being walked by others, any one of which may exert influences upon anyone else, either directly or via 2nd and 3rd order effects. These influences may be persistent or highly time-sensitive, causing even an identical process, if restarted, to yield different results when timed to avoid said influence.

To humans, these branches exist as hypotheticals, “thought experiments”, and fiction, being unreachable, and untestable. To ICOM-based systems, all of the required data, system state, and so on, may be saved, adjusted, and restarted at the target point, allowing for any points of particular interest to be explored along more than a single path, and this process could even be carried out in parallel across divergent branches given some preparation.

The clean and easy transferability of data, metadata, and related context of ICOM-based systems also greatly increases the ease of designing systems of Collective Intelligence around them. This removes risks of miscommunications, echo chambers, and the friction caused by expending greater effort to communicate these matters between humans.

By removing these common sources of degradation, it becomes both feasible and practical to solve far more complex problems, with far greater fidelity, efficacy, and speed. As with the example above, the number of dimensions across which such paths may be explored also takes another leap forward.

Across the past ~1.5 billion years of evolution, since mitochondria joined with bacteria to form the first Eukaryotic cells, upon which virtually all plant and animal life on Earth are built, one pattern has persisted. Each increasing level of complexity is met with increasing levels of cooperation (and specialization). Taken in the context of the Lindy Effect, you may expect another ~1.5 billion years of the same.
