Common Combinatorial Failure Modes

600November 2, 2026

For physical buildings, one person designs the building, another designs the parts, another plans the interiors, another carries out the work, and every once in a while someone else even comes along afterward to check where each of those people fucked up, either in the work itself or in coordination and communication. Even that is a heavily abridged and simplified list, and yet within that short list there are multiple types of failure modes that repeat across their more extensive counterparts.

The architect and the designers of furniture, appliances, fixtures, etc., are two parties who virtually never communicate or coordinate. Even the interior planners only rarely speak with those designers, instead leaving the designers to the whims of parties with little or no actual role in the process, like “focus groups” or corporate executives.

This virtual void of communication, dominantly populated with biased BS, means that those parts being designed must either have some clearly defined and universal ideal performance and form, or they’ll realistically never reach that form barring freakishly unlikely random chance. The result is that for most such products designed today there is a vast gaping maw between “what is” and “what is optimal” for any given purpose.

Many in business would tend to disregard this divide, no matter how vast it may be, because they’re familiar with the human tendency of “satisficing”, where people are simply satisfied with the best option they’re offered, even if all of the offered options are objectively terrible. However, that abruptly changes when a significantly better option is added to the pool of offerings, as the relativistic comparisons of satisficing suddenly paint a very different picture of all other options therein.

Another common problem comes into play where communication is present and consistent, but not clear or complete. Miscommunication like this is common between layers like a designer and the people carrying out the work, in part because that process is often treated as a one-way line of communication, like placing an order, which assumes that the designer has more complete knowledge of how the work can and can’t be performed than they actually do in most real-world cases.

For example, in my travels around the world I’ve seen many of the same types of “trendy” interior designs everywhere, but it also quickly becomes clear where the local skilled labor was lacking, or simply didn’t know what to do with something, or how it might need to be treated or installed differently than more common local trends. This leads to the same expense for parts, but poor installation and increased maintenance for them, only remedied over years and decades as a slow learning curve progresses locally, and only in regions where the latter party is eventually held to some account.

When there is an absence of communication, especially when combined with cultivated marketing (like “focus groups”) and corporate bullshit, or when there is unclear, incomplete, or a naïve attempt at one-way communication, there will be failure, and likely more than one.

None of these roles can or should attempt to operate in a vacuum. Instead, all require clear, consistent, and complete two-way communication, wherein groups of the parties involved may iterate, applying collective intelligence toward paving the way to some more optimal outcome.

In software development, some places have managed to make (limited) progress toward this end. A common example is the “two pizza” teams that Amazon became known for, limiting the size of each team to reduce the burden of that communication between all members to more reasonable levels.

This is one of the limits of human cognitive bandwidth in action, that we can only maintain such levels of communication up to a certain scale, beyond which cognitive bias takes a leading role and collective intelligence collapses into “Groupthink”, or otherwise falls into obscurity.

In-built (and generally punishment-free) processes of iteration are also critical to any pursuit of optimality, with such iteration being the norm across much of the software development space. The iteration must remain largely painless in order to flow freely, because humans are emotional decision-makers, and they will tend to avoid pain.

You also generally don’t want only the subset who don’t avoid pain (aka “Masochists”) to be the only people iterating on a given process. Their counterparts at Microsoft (aka “Sadists”) tend to be the only people truly on-board with the results of the former.

Software development still has plenty of room for improvement, with one example being a very typical reliance on “KPIs” (Key Performance Indicators), which are inherently a subset of the bigger picture, blind to uncertainties and 2nd or 3rd order effects outside of their narrow scope. Optimization over such narrow goals predictably leads to Dystopian and openly malevolent tech companies, following the pattern of a Groupthink lead by bad actors.

The tech industry also tends to directly bar the gates against competence, constructing hiring practices that run polar opposite to collective intelligence, instead optimizing for Groupthink. Microsoft’s contractors are told that they might get hired, but only if they “toe-the-line” of Microsoft’s corporate BS.

Likewise, Amazon’s potential employees famously had to run the gauntlet of getting approval from 7 different current employees, some of them random, any one of whom could prevent them from being hired, which also subsequently cultivated sycophancy and random uncertainties. That problem is only exacerbated by the further layers of gating, like hiring most of their employees from a 3rd party company that steals a large chunk of the potential employee’s pay check (often ~50%), leaving the subset of people stupid enough to get thoroughly ripped off as the majority of those funnelling through their hiring pipeline.

I can relate my personal experience of having an AWS director literally chase me to the door of an event I was leaving, because I asked a question that struck him earlier that evening. He personally scheduled an interview to try recruiting me immediately thereafter, but even his strong and obvious desire to bring me onboard wasn’t sufficient to overcome the gates barring competence from entering the company.

As most real-world multi-person projects require a variety of different skill sets and specialized knowledge to come together, each of those also represents an opportunity for Collective Intelligence systems, be they purely human groups, or groups assisted (or populated) by ICOM-based systems. The degree to which a given group is able to iterate collectively then governs how far they can progress along the paths toward some more optimal outcome than the members could ever realistically reach under more normal levels of communication observed in practice today.

Many problems also exacerbate communication issues, causing it to become even more lossy than the norm, including natural communication barriers like language and culture. As humans can’t share their thoughts or perspectives directly and completely, rather relying on the extremely lossy forms of spoken and written language, these barriers can’t be fully overcome between humans, only reduced.

This issue of lossy communication, especially with regard to natural communication barriers, is also something I’ve written about in the past through our research papers on the ICOM architecture, as graph-based systems can actually share their thoughts both directly and completely between one another. The net result is that while human-to-human communication can’t overcome those natural communication barriers, human-to-ICOM-to-ICOM-to-Human can overcome those same barriers by aligning the ICOM instances in question to their respective human parties.

It is true that most ignore as many of these failure modes as they can get away with, and that what they can get away with has much to do with the levels of satisficing present, but that is also only the norm. Introduce one obviously and significantly superior option to the table, and you may observe the rest being swept away with both vigor and enthusiasm.
