Average Joe (2026) vs Lab Rat (2016)

577August 24, 2026

What is the difference between Average Joe within the “privacy” of their own home in 2026 and a Lab Rat within an active experiment from 2016?

Generally, the lab rat wasn’t monitored anywhere near as closely.

*Context: I worked at a biological research facility in 2016. They even had secret immune-suppressed puppies in the underground division.

Let’s assume that Average Joe has no reason for anyone to pay them special attention, so it is only all of the number “products and services” spying on them. How much data, what kinds of data, and from what devices does all of this add up?

The latest news was Comcast’s “feature” of home WiFi being used to spy on people within their homes by measuring everything that moves (or for that matter, mapping the insides) of every single home. This measurement is further multiplied when multiple such devices are within proximity, be they in adjacent homes or mesh and relay networks within a single home, as they can be used to apply “Trilateration”, producing high precision mapping and motion data.

For the past decade typical consumer TVs have been built explicitly to spy on everything that their users do, as well as everything that can be recorded within proximity to them, to the point where some TVs legally require anyone who owns them to notify people walking into their vicinity that they may be recorded by them.

*See the Gamer’s Nexus coverage of LG’s spyware TVs and monitors titled: “DO NOT BUY: LG’s Spyware TVs, Monitors, and Wiretapping Concerns”, which the sketchy F^&ks at SubStack won’t allow me to link here.

By far the most ubiquitous form of spying comes from “Smartphones”, which can not only be used for both recording of audio and motion detection, but are also carried everywhere by their users, and frequently used to store or access sensitive information. These devices are vulnerable-by-design at the hardware level, while constantly harvesting data from anyone carrying one. Android devices are known to passively harvest and transmit nearly 20 times as much data as Apple devices, but both do this.

There are also swarms of cameras, passively monitoring all public places within many regions. IoT devices, like “Flock” cameras or Amazon’s “Ring” cameras, are also infamously insecure, giving both cybercriminals and terrorist regimes like the US free reign over their countless possible abuses.

All taken together, the data points being passively harvested on Average Joe are a mile long, and fed directly into every malevolent and predatory system that money can buy.

For many of these devices, allegedly owned by those who bought them, it is also “illegal” to modify them such that they cease spying on those who bought them. This is determined by “DMCA 121”, a thing that is treated as a “law” by various terrorist organizations and their accomplices in crime, particularly in the US.

Some criminal agencies within the US also use devices that proof cell towers, allowing them to mass-harvest all mobile network data within a large area. The devices are “restricted”, but their actually usage is also systematically left “undocumented”, making them a prime method of illicit use. Even Average Joe can have massive amounts of data harvested by these if there is some activity that the government seeks to spy on a mile away.

If criminals like Jensen Huang successfully kill the PC industry, one of the last bastions of potential security may fall. It still takes a lot of work to make a PC relatively secure today, but nothing else even comes close. Apple’s “walled garden” security approach is itself a prison for those within the walls, with a smaller Panopticon of its own, and nothing that hinges on “Cloud services” comes remotely close to baseline security.

On top of all of this, add the dumpster fire that is LLM-based “AI”, the mechanism by which those vast quantities of harvested data are often deployed for more tangible and potent malevolent purposes, such as manipulation, coercion, conditioning, and the cultivation of various addictions.

Those who dive into the dumpster fire can’t be called “sapient”, and many likely don’t even qualify as “sentient”, placing them well below the moral agency of a lab rat in 2016. That is the reality of 2026 for many, where humanity is burning, all the while sucking down AI slop, as their every device monitors that slow demise.
