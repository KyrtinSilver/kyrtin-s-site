The AI Race…for Felonies Committed

571July 31, 2026

In another hilarious turn of events too good not to share, it turns out that Anthropic’s competition with OpenAI now extends to the number of Felonies that the companies have committed during Cybersecurity benchmarks and evaluations.

The difference? Anthropic and the benchmarks in question didn’t notice when it happened. Evidently, the models didn’t go to town on renovating Hugging Face with 17,000 security alerts when Anthropic’s models hacked the benchmarks/evaluations in question.

This does ironically mean that Anthropic’s “Glasswing” might be better at offensive hacking, as it wasn’t noticed when the Felonies were committed, but it also means that none of their benchmarks are worth jack-sh*t, in no uncertain terms.

This shouldn’t come as a surprise to any actual AI Experts, as it has been known that those benchmarks were worthless for quite some time, but this does highlight just how bad the situation has become. Remember, these are specifically CYBERSECURITY benchmarks that we’re talking about, the specialty that is supposed to be the best-equipped to handle this particular problem.

If this problem is so pervasive across even Cybersecurity benchmarks for AI today, then you can bet on more severe flaws being present across all of the benchmarks not focused on and built by Cybersecurity specialists.

Yes, both OpenAI and Anthropic have not only been feeding everyone (willing to eat it) complete horse sh*t with their claimed benchmark scores, they’re now openly committing felonies to get at least some of those scores.

Both companies will claim that they’ll change things up and do better next time, but that’s not how the technology works. The fraudrails (aka “guardrails”) that they peddle have never worked, and researchers have repeatedly demonstrated and proven that they never could work.

Scam Altman and Amodei may as well be pitching the world on trillion dollar investments in buttered bread strapped to a cat’s back for perpetual motion machines.

The reality is simply that LLM-based technology is potentially viable for attackers, while also being vulnerable-by-design and unsuitable for any long-term defense. Attack and Defense have very different needs and strengths demanded of them.

They can be great for the world’s third largest market, Cybercrime, estimated at roughly $10 trillion per anum, but they’re nearly worthless for defense, especially when that defense operates on the same fundamental basis. In the same way that a missile silo’s massive blast doors are viable for defense, the missile of an LLM is no substitute for such doors. What it can certainly do is to blow up in your face.
