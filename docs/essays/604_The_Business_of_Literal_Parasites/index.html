The Business of Literal Parasites

604November 7, 2026

Most pet owners in the US are familiar with treatments given to their pets once every 6 months, intended to prevent various kinds of parasites, such as many kinds of intestinal worms. Even such standard treatments are more than 3 times more expensive in the US than in Vietnam and other similar countries, for the exact same product (even with the same English-language instructions). This is made worse by preventing anyone other than Veterinarians from buying them directly, even at 3x and above inflated prices.

More ironic still is that in Vietnam it is a standard practice of good hygiene for humans to take medicine that kills parasites once every 6 months (less than $1 in cost, named “Fugacar”), something that is entirely alien as a concept in many, if not all, of the world’s richest countries. When I attempted to make a price comparison with the US for such products I quickly realized that the US (and similar countries) make it impossible for such anti-parasitic treatments to become normal and societally encouraged hygiene.

The problem is that they place doctors and their prescriptions as gatekeepers for such medications, which both dramatically inflates the cost (on substances that can’t realistically be abused) as well as adding layers of pointless red tape, greatly increasing the wasted time required. Doctors in such countries in turn also present the distinct problem that they don’t typically prescribe truly preventative medicine, no matter how logical and scientifically-backed doing so may be, as is the case for intestinal parasites.

This was put to the test with someone in this region I’ve met who originates from Australia, another individual who confirmed that absence of such a preventative medical norm in that country. In his case, he also had the most alarming personal story of relevance, where a family member had contracted an intestinal parasite of especially acute risk, where it quickly grew to ~15kg, significantly larger than a baby, and had to be removed at a hospital. Even with such a rare horror story in his personal knowledge, he had no concept of taking preventative medicine himself up to the point of it being explicitly recommended to him.

For countries like the US and Australia where medical treatment is heavily symptom-dependent, and virtually never (credibly) preventative in nature, problems like such parasites tend to go unnoticed and/or unrecognized until they become absolutely extreme, like a 15 kg parasite. Far more common within such medically neglected regions is for many symptoms that can’t be untangled from other sources to crop up (like untreated intestinal parasites), becoming co-morbidities to all other health problems that an individual may have.

No amount of antidepressants or ADHD medication is going to get rid of those parasites, and those parasites are going to make other health problems worse, as they deprive the human body of one importance source of nutrition or another, while producing their own harmful chemical by-products. Some of those intestinal parasites also directly cause ulcers, inflammation, or cancer, as was the case for a few noted in research found here: https://www.sciencealert.com/parasites-thrive-in-the-us-despite-what-many-americans-think

As noted in that research article: “For instance, in many countries the drugs needed to treat hookworm infections cost mere cents, but in the US, where drug prices are unregulated by the federal government, these same medications can cost hundreds of dollars.”

Anyone who didn’t have their head planted very deep up their own ass during the dawn of Covid-19 should have some concept of how easily parasites, bacteria, and viruses can spread across the world. This is every bit as true of intestinal parasites as it is of many viruses and bacteria, meaning that any virus, bacteria, or parasite present in a large globalized city with a population in the millions and a working airport (and export business) can easily become globally distributed. Even the often extreme measures taken to try and prevent the spread of Covid-19 failed miserably, and no such measures are taken to prevent the vast majority of parasites from spreading globally today.

Besides the various valid comparisons that may be drawn between parasites and many politicians in general, acute examples like RJK Jr. taking his family out to swim in sewage-contaminated water, as he governs the US’s “Health and Human Services”, point to the severity of the status quo. The US may have noticed the outbreak of explosive diarrhea, but they just as predictably fail to notice increasing risks of parasitic infection and all medical complications caused by those parasites.

This cultivates the ironic divide between countries who’ve risen out of poverty in recent decades and those that enjoyed the most wealth throughout. Those who truly developed often presently benefit from healthier populations than the countries that fermented over the same period (or that went bad, as the case may be).

Many simple preventative measures can resolve health problems before they take root, such as balancing salt and potassium intake, iron supplements, routine anti-parasitics, and as-needed mainline antibiotics, but such prevention is only possible in the absence of gatekeepers and red tape. The gatekeepers and red tape keep entire populations sick and confined to downward spirals, no matter how false the majority of their claims in favor of doing so may be.

An important aspect of autonomy is the right to dictate your own health, or neglect thereof as the case may be, which no government on the planet may claim the slightest authority over. In such matters of autonomy, the highest authority is the individual, even if the individual defers that authority to their intestinal and brain parasites respectively, as is the case for RFK Jr.

Republican or Democrat, the US and similar countries remain highly pro-parasite, in the literal medical sense. Though your ideologies may be divided, your intestinal parasites stand united.
