Computing Direct Responsibility

592October 24, 2026

To first define the term, “Direct Responsibility” may be defined as any outcome for which all determining factors may be traced back to a given entity, event, choice, etc.

“Determining factors” are distinguished from “Influential factors”, where determining factors decide if an outcome occurs or not, and influential factors are unable to change the outcome, but may influence the specific severity and/or aspects of that outcome. In other words, determining factors select the outcome, and influential factors modify the attributes of that outcome.

This means that “Direct Responsibility” can and does exist in the real world, as it only requires responsibility for the determining factors, not the sum of all possible factors (avoiding requiring omniscience). This responsibility sets the baseline positive or negative ethical value that may be attributed to any given action, with influential factors producing a secondary fuzzy and potentially complex space, partly based upon the fraction of those influences that were predictable, among other interacting factors.

Some of that secondary fuzzy and potentially complex space can be refined and quantified, such as distinguishing which influences were or weren’t possible or feasible to mitigate, beyond the ability to predict them.

For example, it is entirely predictable that the judge overseeing Luigi Mangioni’s case will make entirely unethical decisions (as the laws dictate), just as it is entirely predictable that said judge has a home where they sleep at night, absent the ability to mitigate a single 50 caliber round. In that case, the factors are predictable, and the cost is not only feasible, but trivial (1 round plus bleach), not counting entirely reusable equipment costs. The same is true with regard to disposing of infamous war criminals and their henchmen, whose personal details are sold by numerous “data brokers” today, by methods that even comedians have publicly demonstrated.

(See the 2022 episode “Data Brokers: Last Week Tonight with John Oliver”)

Part of the measure of feasibility has to account for tactics, which Sun Tzu understood and famously wrote on the subject of, pointing out how defenses vary, and how even defenses on all sides pose their own kinds of weakness. For every criminal that a government employs who wears body armor during the day, they tend to go home and take that armor off at night, and they usually engage in no shortage of other routines and habits where no such protection is present. What isn’t feasible at 12 noon may be perfectly feasible at midnight, leaving the net result feasible.

Direct and Indirect Responsibility may be easily visualized as streams of events, with one color of stream for deterministic, and another for influential. If the deterministic tributaries for one event all trace back to a given point, that point holds direct responsibility, and if it traces back to several, both hold some indirect responsibility for it.

Likewise, full and direct deterministic responsibility may be attributed to multiple points in a stream, at every point where all deterministic tributaries converge, since responsibility isn’t zero-sum. Attempts to circumvent the consequences of that responsibility, whether a given individual attempts to dodge the consequences of their own actions or prevent another from facing their own responsibilities, are examples where a carbon copy of those consequences is produced, multiplying punishment for the former, and applying a carbon copy of the attempted prevention to the perpetrator of the latter.

The influential streams are worth noting, and may themselves be useful for mitigating the consequences of some problems, but they can’t realistically change the determined outcome at a more fundamental level, at least not short of superintelligence architecting their confluences for such a purpose. Even the lesser form of architecting for mitigation via such influences is complex and rarely seen in practice today.

As is the repeating pattern within scientific progress in general, a large and growing subset of Responsibility in general may be computed, especially where Direct Responsibility is concerned. As time moves forward, we may thus predict that this pattern continues, with an ever-shrinking subset of fuzzy and incomputable responsibilities.
