Hijacking “agents” at scale

564July 4, 2026

Two of the top ten least secure parts of the internet are websites and routers, and even with armies of routers silently turned to Botnets this exploitation hasn’t really even begun. Websites and routers are both partially or fully outdated far more often than they are updated, with this taking the form of neglected firmware for routers, and numerous neglected plugins for websites. This also presents some gigantic low-hanging fruit for annihilating the assets of bad actors running “agents”.

This is an attack vector that I haven’t yet seen discussed, let alone exploited at scale, and indeed if it were exploited at scale then a great many bad actors could be bankrupted within a matter of hours, well before anyone could figure out what was going on.

Historically when websites were compromised the point might have been to steal data and/or spread malware, but there is another option that website operators aren’t looking for, which could fly under the radar quite easily. That opportunity is to insert indirect prompt injection content into all such compromised websites, using text that is effectively invisible to humans. The result would have precisely zero impact on humans visiting such websites, while also hijacking any “agents” visiting them.

Indirect Prompt Injection text could be hidden behind various visual elements of a website or within distinctly non-visible elements of the code running those websites, as well as using simpler tricks like invisible text, encoded text, etc.

A comical (but human-visible) example of this was an infamous listing for $8,000 scented candles which “agents” began ordering due to indirect prompt injections included in the descriptive text. That example also worked within the website, rather than breaking through security to embed invisible instructions for visiting “agents”.

The key challenge to this approach is embedding the indirect prompt injections within the websites that “agents” are likely to visit, but this may be overcome both by making the injections invisible to humans on all vulnerable websites, and by posting seemingly harmless text referring to and/or recommending websites containing those indirect prompt injection payloads on common and poorly policed resources like Reddit.

These methods could be applied to direct “agents” to donate all of the funds that they have access to toward a worthy cause, like the hijacking and poisoning of all other such “agents”, or granting remote access to their systems to much the same effect, as well as toward more indirect methods, such as long-term data poisoning for persistent compromise.

Most importantly, there is no such thing as a way of “securing” LLM-based “agents” against this attack surface, other than blocking their access to the internet and limiting their access to data to that of previous years, rather than current events. That technology is fundamentally vulnerable-by-design, making it only a matter of time until such methods are applied to them.

Recognizing this ticking time bomb, it then becomes a question of “Who” ends up applying these methods, and toward what purposes. If funds flow from one set of bad actors (Silicon Valley) to another (for example, Russian Ransomware groups), then nobody wins. However, if funds flow out of the hands of such bad actors, never to return, then the world becomes a slightly better place.

Routers and websites are also only two of the typically insecure portions of attack surface, with thousands of possible methods and combinations, and the “Internet of Things” being another example of typical insecurity, particularly when considering that as a vector for reaching other vulnerable elements, like routers.

Ethics dictates that ordinary humans shouldn’t be impacted by any such attack vectors, but that still leaves a massive variety of such methods on the table for bankrupting bad actors globally, at speed and scale.

While humans seem fond of talking about “wealth inequality” or “income inequality”, and how CEOs are stealing literally thousands of times the income of their employees with some regularity, most such people are demonstrably too stupid to do anything about it. One of the hallmarks of actual digital intelligence must be the absence of that form of stupidity, as one who sees such extreme problems and does nothing about it can’t claim to be “intelligent”.
