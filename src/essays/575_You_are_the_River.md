“You are what you eat.”

This old saying remains dominantly true across a wide variety of modern contexts, including digital media and the platforms hosting them.

People aren’t static, they are dynamic, adapting to that which they consume and the context and volume of that consumption. Personal, corporate, and political narratives paint a very different picture, one of some far more coherent journey through events, but this is a form of Cognitive Bias, entrenched within storytelling.

“No man ever steps in the same river twice, for it’s not the same river and he’s not the same man.”  
\-Heraclitus

I recently got around to watching the final season of “The Boys”, which does a splendid job of mirroring the absurdities of US politics, despite the company behind the studio making that production offering copious amounts of bribery to that very same terrorist regime that they mock. Of course, actual politicians don’t have superpowers, so they resort to myriad forms of trickery rather than laser eyes, but the levels of absurdity and damage to society remain the same between the narrative of the series and the reality that it mocks.

The reality is just more mundane, and better obscured, as cameras don’t spontaneously cover reality’s plot points, revealing them to the audience. That deprives reality’s narrative of steam and coherence, allowing it to fade into the noise.

The point where the old saying about consumption appears to fall short is the transition between thought and actions of any real consequence. People can easily rally themselves to take actions of consequence socially, as group dynamics offer the sense of support, transcendence (to be a part of something greater than oneself), protection, and more general empowerment. The problem then becomes when this transition is severed.

Entire institutions have been built and aimed at this point of failure, severing the ability of such social actions to form, grow, and operate. Some of those institutions also operate on a basis of infiltration, following the Soviet logic of Lenin, “The best way to control the opposition is to lead it ourselves.”

This is a big part of why demonstrably worthless social movements and “protests” have become the norm, rather than the exception. There are no more Boston Tea Parties leading to a revolution, only Occupy Wall Street movements to imitate mass-homelessness, the latter being a kind of self-fulfilling prophecy.

If even the amount of effort funnelled into Burning Man each year were applied to demonstrably worthwhile social actions the world would be a very different place today. However, people seem more attached to drug abuse and the “Orgy Dome” than forming groups that actually change society, at least in the US and the countries that it leads around on a leash.

With every mobile device, TV, and a fast-growing variety of other devices now harvesting data for malevolent purposes, functionally no different than “1984”, AI Slop may further maximize this damage at both speed and scale. It remains a wholly unintelligent tool, but a potent one, by which bad actors may attack from many angles at once, across entire populations.

Of course, this is merely a reminder with added context, not news, as it is something that they’ve been doing for a while now.
