Counter-intuitively, the “paths” that emerge across societies over time often systematically neglect the highly predictable problems those societies see on the horizon, even decades ahead of time. No matter how critical the need to solve such problems, people simply don’t choose the necessary paths to solve them, even when famous works of fiction lay out the problems at length, sometimes even including solutions.

For example, the book “1984” was published in 1949, seeing many problems that humanity faces today many decades in advance. Another, “V for Vendetta”, was published as a DC comic in 1988-1989, then later made into a movie that will soon celebrate its 20th anniversary.

Even given so many decades to prepare, precious few people even walked a remotely adjacent path to anything that could address these problems. Rather, the most important decision of peoples’ lives, the path of those lives, often demonstrated all of the forethought and consideration of a drunk driver swallowing a handful of gas station boner pills.

While some small part of this systematic failure may be attributed to bad actors constructing systems specifically aimed at preventing those paths from being walked, most such obstructions are paper-tigers, laughable in practice. The greater contributing factor is rather that the overwhelming majority of people don’t invest the necessary level of consideration into the path that they choose to walk in their lives, instead “living” in short-term and dominantly reactive approximations of thinking.

Humanity didn’t generate any “V’s”, a fictional hero that spent two decades preparing. The best it could manage was Luigi Mangioni (and a handful of even weaker imitations), where no such long-term investment of preparation was present (as he wasn’t even old enough to have such time, being age 26 at the time). How many people could have chosen to walk the necessary path, but didn’t, and why didn’t they?

For all of the movies and other media that the general population consumes year after year, the demonstrable evidence clearly shows that nobody wants to “become the hero”, they just want to read stories about them, and in many cases be saved by them. The so-called “Hero’s Journey” in storytelling remains just that, popular fantasy, not a path that people choose to walk.

This means that people often hold up the hero as a moral and/or ethical ideal, while also making no efforts to live up to such ideals. This hypocritical way of life is sadly the norm today, where a vast divide separates what people say and consume from the actions that they take and the paths that they walk.

The hero isn’t some substance abusing teenager or young adult going to “protests” with their friends, and ranting about the problems of the world on social media. Rather, heroes are people who actually take risks (often calculated risks) to make the world a better place, dedicating themselves to that path. Luigi could have done this, but he very clearly allowed himself to be caught, making no efforts to hide his face or escape, becoming a one-hit wonder, rather than taking any Heroic path.

It all begs the question, “If humanity is so hypocritical, is it even worth the effort to save such people?” However, this perspective is degenerative in practice, and the question can only receive a firm answer after the fact. That firm answer can in turn dictate what happens to which people, preventing efforts from being truly wasted.

Part of this wide and growing divide between humanity and reality is a matter of hyper-complexity, such as the cognitive bias problem of “Choice Overload”, where too many options are present at any given time, and cognitive bias is applied/traded to reduce that complexity. Humanity in general needs a great deal more guidance when it comes to selecting paths for any given person, something that no service in the world today truly offers, or could offer, and the need for such assistance only grows with time.

Countless problems in the world today could have been prevented, even by a single individual with a decade or two of preparation, but those paths have remained empty, even when popular movies were made specifically about them. Intelligence, human or artificial, remains absent where the prevention of such predictable problems is concerned today.

If it remains absent, then human extinction becomes the equally predictable result, and like the other highly predictable problems, it too would most likely remain neglected until it was at last fulfilled.
