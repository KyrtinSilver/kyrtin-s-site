The “Penny Rounding” scheme of shaving the rounded pennies off of every transaction was famously entrenched in pop culture by Superman III (1983), which was later re-iterated in Office Space (but with incompetence in implementation), further raising the level of popular recognition. Someone or some group who adds little or no value to a process engages in a middle-man attack, scraping money from all transactions within their reach.

The irony is that few people seem to consciously recognize that this scheme is implemented globally, and at Office Space levels of incompetence, across numerous industries today. This “Penny Rounding” treatment also applies not only to monetary transactions, but perhaps more importantly to the time, cognitive capacities, integrity, and security of nearly every globalized process that one could name today.

Rather than only being a means of theft, to take something that is desired (absent right or merit), this treatment can also take the form of offloading something that is undesirable (absent justification or logical reason). For example, many of the “Bullshit Jobs” that David Graeber’s research covered exist partly or fully because something undesirable has been offloaded to a group who has no reason to carry that burden.

A common pattern of this takes the form of one government department or corporate division chasing after some KPI or arbitrary goal, blind to the consequences of their own actions, wherein any consequences are then pushed onto another department or division, often in additional to many or all of their citizens, customers, and/or clients. Many modern “immigration” processes have followed this pattern, where multiple departments and divisions each throw their own piles of horse shit on top of any adjacent departments and divisions, all of it piling up with little or no recourse for anyone buried under all of that shit.

However, government and corporate employees do get paid to perform those Bullshit Jobs, giving them a limited umbrella of mitigation. The people with no mitigation, and usually no substantive recourse to speak of, are everyone else, and everyone else also generally gets hit with those same piles of shit.

1.  1 hour of wasted time (per citizen)
    
2.  $25 of added (wasted) cost to every citizen, and $5 in processing cost per person to the government
    
3.  A 10% chance of further time and money being wasted (uncertainty)
    
4.  A 1% chance of extremely and/or uniquely bad outcomes (per citizen, uncertainty), markedly degrading the cognitive capacities of 20% of those filling out the forms for the processing period.
    
5.  A 15% chance of a major Cybersecurity breach of that government agency and their collected data (per year)
    
6.  A virtually 100% chance of degrading the integrity of the government in question, as virtually every additional government form with the above attributes serves to highlight the incompetence and/or malevolence of said government.  
    

How often does policy advice consider such a list of consequences, even when they are all both direct and predictable? This isn’t even mentioning the 2nd and 3rd order consequences (some of which remain largely predictable), which in many cases exceed the severity of those noted above.

For that matter, when is the last time you saw someone or some group actually taking responsibility for such a list of consequences? The damage to the integrity of an entire government is far more diffuse and intangible, making this vastly more difficult to package in a form for the repayment of this kind of debt, but a meaningful start is to reduce to integrity of the offending Directors to zero, preventing them from being allowed to make any such decisions moving forward.
