Between what a company or domain claims to be and do, versus what what they actually are and do, there lies a divide that may be vast and deep. This divide is a line that separates naivety from observation, as one cares about claims and levels of trust, while the other observes the visible subsets of reality.

While the field of “AI” could be an easy punching bag for this topic, other fields of note include “News” and “Advertising” in particular. These domains aren’t homogeneous, so there are scattered pockets where the claims and actions of groups and individuals do actually align, but by both volume and market cap these are the exceptions, not the rule.

In the case of “News” (Journalism), the claim is often that an essential public service is provided, keeping people up-to-date with current events. For most of what these companies actually do today, this is obviously far from accurate.

Most “News” is rather an endless stream of trivia, an assortment of somewhat random and sometimes maliciously biased trash being thrown at every accessible eyeball. This mirrors the “attention economy” of social media in some ways, where it is a struggle to maximize attention, not to discover and deliver any meaningful “news”.

Silence is something with tremendous and often forgotten value. It is every bit as important to say nothing when there is nothing meaningful to be said, as it is to say that which actually matters.

Attempts at attention maximization systematically aim to deplete Silence, sharply and cumulatively degrading the cognitive capacities of all that they influence in doing so. Such maximization is also fundamentally misaligned with the stated claims of most companies and domains, making all claims built upon them inherently false.

A noteworthy exception within the “News” domain that has persisted for more than 20 years is that of “Comedy News”, where the claims are different, focused on sharing bits of news for comedic purposes. Such shows also air no more than once per day, and sometimes only once per week, avoiding the pitfalls of trash news where trivia and baseless speculation more often take center stage.

Comedy News is able to live up to the claims that it makes, while providing a broadly appreciated public service, even as the overwhelming majority of other “News” does nothing of the sort. It highlights current events and places them in a funny light (on average), which has the desirable by-product of informing people, while also making them laugh.

The “Advertising” domain is another example worth Sparta Kicking off of the nearest cliff. Companies within it generally claim to benefit both their client companies and the consumers thereof, no matter how obviously false those claims may be to anyone with basic math and critical thinking skills.

Part of the façade is built upon antiquated perceptions of how “consumers” use technology to make decisions, as that has changed a great deal over the past several decades. While advertising might have played a more credible role in the 1950s, in the modern age those same people being subjected to ads are generally using digital platforms, search functions with filters, and sometimes recommendation engines.

The difference between these two dynamics is, as noted above, extremely vast. The “Advertiser” (scammer) attempts to tell a (false) story about some product, often giving little or no comparison to other products, except perhaps a heavily biased token gesture. The search engines, filters, and recommendation engines of shopping platforms have their own problems, and those problems are numerous, but one thing they actually tend to do that “Advertising” systematically avoids is to compare not just a subset of products, but all available products.

In more recent years many shopping platforms have mixed the two together, highlighting advertised products (bribery), which in turn sharply degrades any value that their search and recommender system functions may have otherwise offered. This is a prototypical example of how “Advertising” actually functions, where it attempts to maliciously exploit all available vulnerabilities, be they cognitive biases or loopholes in a website’s functionality.

The field of Cognitive Bias research was formalized largely thanks to efforts like those of Daniel Kahneman, Cass R. Sunstein, Richard Thaler, and Amos Tversky, but long before that the field of “Advertising” was systematically exploiting numerous cognitive biases, having already recognized and grabbed a hold of those vulnerabilities within the human mind. They discovered them first, and thoroughly exploited those discoveries.

Even today, the field of “Advertising” may still be credited with either directly creating or contributing strongly to the construction of numerous “Dark Patterns”, by which billions of people are systematically and repeatedly manipulated every day, and across countless platforms. They still pioneer the process of turning malicious and opportunistic intentions into systems of exploitation, which many researchers now make a living studying and writing papers about.

If “Advertising” were designed to deliver the claimed value to both client companies and consumers, then it wouldn’t look anything like it does today. With the possible exception of a small subset of Superbowl advertisements, which tend to take a strongly comedic stance, the overwhelming majority of “Advertising” is HATED in no uncertain terms, and even though that hatred may not be consciously formalized for many, it remains a strong unconscious influence, as the human mind is very good at retaining emotional associations.

Such “Advertising” also costs the client companies money, a cost which is passed on to any consumers stupid enough to buy their products and services. A major example of this is Coca-Cola, whose advertising spend famously reached 50% of their overall expenses as a company, meaning that their every product was twice as expensive as it should have been otherwise, all else being equal. Then again, people have used it as toilet bowl cleaner, and it takes a lot of manipulation (or cocaine) to get people drinking toilet bowl cleaner.

While “News” as a domain has the exception of “Comedy News” as a category, the “Advertising” domain has no real exception to their status quo of malicious intent that I’ve yet come across. This begs the question of what such an exception might look like, were it to be created.

While some Science Fiction worthy example might be imagined (and indeed, ICOM-based systems offer some unique possibilities of their own), a more practical approach is to build upon what exists today in the direction of adding the value that “Advertising” falsely claims to deliver. A few such methods come to mind:

-   Improving and Extending Search and Filter functions across all digital marketplaces. This means giving people more ways of filtering out all of the things that they hate, and that aren’t relevant, as well as highlighting what they seek, to fish them out of a sea of similar products.  
    
-   Improving and allowing for the customization of Recommender systems. This takes the results of search and filter usage a step further, formalizing how those tools are used into a graph structure, where predictions may be made across a connectome of related data points at every point in time, with those predictions checked and refined after the fact. After all, a recommender system that doesn’t dynamically improve in this way tends to be worthless in practice.  
    
-   Dynamically, Systematically, and Continuously verifying the data (including products and services) of your platform. This includes not just keeping watch over the frequent abuse of product rating and review systems, but also performing 3rd party mystery shopper reviews, especially focused on checking products whose listings show signs of rating and review abuse.  
    

For example, Newegg as a platform has a fairly decent set of search functions and filters, allowing users to quickly and easily narrow down the results to what they’re actually looking for. In contrast, Amazon is a shit-show, with only a small fraction of filter options appearing on many searches, and even those functions don’t appear reliably.

Sadly, the other two forms of improvement tend to be extremely neglected in practice today. Recommender systems tend to be naïve Machine Learning, optimized by malicious and/or idiotic criteria, such as the famous Amazon example of “You bought a Toilet seat recently. Here are a dozen other toilet seats available for purchase”.

If Audible (another Amazon company) had even the faintest shred of competence in recommender systems then they’d recognize that I personally have strong violent impulses toward their every employee every single time those vermin recommend Harry Potter books to me, and that hatred never decays, it accumulates, and sharpens.

Verification of the data on platforms is generally a token gesture, often only focusing on pain points that harm the platform’s profitability, treating the rest as infeasible to resolve. In actuality, it is challenging more because it is adversarial, meaning that the company in question must itself be more competent than its adversaries, which often isn’t true.

In the pursuit of “Advertising” revenue many modern platforms have gone so far as to cripple themselves, to the point where their websites simply don’t function at all, if they even manage to load. Rather than making better products and services, they double down on malicious methods and intentions. YouTube, LinkedIn, numerous “News” websites, and countless other platforms now spend much of their time utterly broken, at least to any baseline competent human with Adblockers and Anti-trackers.

Such platforms spend a great deal of effort to break themselves, rather than improving their platforms in ways that might justify paying for them. “Removing Advertising” isn’t such a justification, in the same way that “removing the shit from a cup of coffee” isn’t a valid value proposition.

As it exists today, “Advertising” is both the sewage pipe that transports malicious intentions and methods (including Dark Patterns), as well as the sewage of all clients companies flowing through it. What kind of idiot chooses to let such excrement spray them in the face every time they go to a website?

Many fields in the modern world harbor such vast divides between claims and actuality, “Politics” being another striking example, placing the battle between naivety and observation on many fronts. Cognitive Bandwidth is finite, and humans are built to trade off complexity for cognitive bias to compensate for this, but systems can and have been built to overcome hypercomplexity. The ability to observe many fronts, to see what is happening from many angles absent naïve simplifications and diversions, all at once, is a thing of immense value.
