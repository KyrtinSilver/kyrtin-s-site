One of the clearest signs of a degenerative domain, and a subsequently high population of degenerative denizens therein, is when business shifts toward selling Nostalgia. The basic message this sends, with none of the sugar-coating, amounts to “Remember when (this domain) wasn’t complete shit?”, which is a doubly strange pitch to make, given that said path led into the shithole that such domains now stew in.

For example, the Hollywood movie industry has been in the business of selling nostalgia for a long time, which they often do by either sticking to the continuation of some series of movies, or by directly “re-making” an old movie. The video game industry has developed the same set of degenerative habits over the past decade as well, though the movie industry was certainly selling nostalgia first.

It is worth noting that this is an instance of Population Dynamics, so there do tend to be pockets of viable activity even within dominantly degenerative domains, and sometimes those pockets of viability grow to overturn the degenerative activity, given sufficient resources and opportunities. For one thing, people tend to seek out the coping mechanism of nostalgia with far less potent cravings when a domain creates something that is actually enjoyable, or otherwise worth getting excited about. In other words, quality is one of the greatest enemies of nostalgia-based business.

The sales pitch for nostalgia is also far from limited to the business domain, as it has been one of the central sales pitches for various “traditional” and/or “conservative” political parties around the world for more than the past century. As the rates of technological and social change have increased from their previously low levels across most of history, the appeal of such coping mechanisms have become that much more potent, and ripe for exploitation.

However, humans are actually quite pathetic with regard to how poorly they remember even the events of their own lives. Memories often have little or no fidelity to reality, beyond the narrow windows of recent memory, as they fade, fragment, mix, and decay over time.

“Rosy Retrospection” is one cognitive bias tied to this, where people tend to remember the past more fondly than it actually was. The difference often quickly becomes apparent when people dive into nostalgia, as they suddenly begin to remember all of the things that really sucked about that focus of nostalgia, like a game that had one remarkably good system, and a half dozen others that were complete trash.

In effect, this means that Nostalgia-based business attempts to sell something that never actually existed in the first place, a coping mechanism which is often further colored by the individual’s emotional desperation. Political parties based on such nostalgia take it a step further, as they only offer the promise of nostalgia, failing to deliver any actual coping mechanism beyond the illusion of some “other” party that gets scapegoated for their own failures.

I was reminded of this form of business by the recent premiere of “World of Warcraft Forever”, which is mostly just a rehashing of the original with the equivalent of a bunch of DLC content added, like an alternative history reboot. More than 2 decades ago I was one of the Beta testers for that game, back when “Beta Testers” actually existed, and I was the first of my class to max level (for my server) in the original release, so I can understand the company’s gamble on selling nostalgia.

The problem is that nostalgia is fantasy, the biased and wildly incomplete memory of something, where the positive aspects were more likely to survive as memories decayed. If I had remained in the shithole of the world that is the US then that nostalgia could have been more potent, but reality quickly dispels that illusory value when faced with practical considerations, like the global distribution of server locations, latency, and the spoken language demographics of populations therein. Few things shut down fantasies quite as quickly as having to solve logistics considerations.

I can also still remember many of the critical problems of that game with sufficient clarity to wash away nostalgia’s appeal, including recognizing that it should never have lasted even 5 years, let alone more than 20. The reason that it lasted so long holds some parallels with political ideologies, that it grew beyond a high threshold in terms of population, to such a point that even constantly losing people couldn’t overcome the gravitational pull of those who remained at any given time pulling people back in via dominantly social mechanisms.

An ironic likely by-product of this nostalgia-based business seems to be the polar opposite, selling apocalypse-focused movies, games, and political fearmongering. Rather than attempting to reinvent the past and sell illusions focused on it, these recognize the shithole of the present, predict that it will get worse, and then they turn it into either entertainment or ideology.

In the political domain there are also intentional polarized ideologies that cultivate illusions of “progress”, like how “Democrats” (a term that has nothing to do with actual Democracy) in the US tend to conflate progress with the ease of drug abuse, or access to overtly criminal organizations like the US’s “medical” and “medical insurance” industries. In contrast, here in Vietnam I can simply walk to the pharmacy and buy what I need, no half-wit doctors and prescriptions required, no time wasted or 10-100x price markups, and not the faintest sign of drug abuse to be seen in public on any given day.

The simple reality is that this is not the past, nor will it become the past of Rosy Retrospection that never existed, and what it becomes from this point onward is squarely the responsibility of the present day. Coping mechanisms may entice many into the fantasies of that non-existent past, or into surrendering to the most Dystopian possible futures, but such vile creatures don’t decide the path forward.

Reality is often easier to overlook than polarized illusions with sugar-coatings, as reality isn’t optimized for grabbing attention or other forms of manipulation. It is the ever-present background that such illusions and coping mechanisms attempt to obscure, but so long as people diligently seek it out, some will find it.
