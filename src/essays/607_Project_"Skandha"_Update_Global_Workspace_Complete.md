As we close in on the end of 2026, my colleague and I are focused on completing and testing the project before year’s end. The seed material, basic infrastructure, and Global Workspace segments are all complete, with the remaining segment newly simplified to help us reach this target for the deployment of version 1 to Open Source.

This should place us at around ~83.3% completion now, with my colleague’s (related) thesis complete, presented, and his focus now back on our remaining workload. I also appreciate his significant contributions now more than ever, having seen first-hand how many people contribute virtually nothing to open source projects.

Originally (earlier this year), I had estimated the project to take 4-8 weeks to complete, though that assumed one segment per person, and that each person actually contributed, with no extended breaks. I can now safely say that 3 out of the 4 segments were indeed within that range, with the Global Workspace being a bit bulkier and closer to 12 weeks of consistent work, although the assumption of locating 3 other viable project contributors proved false.

As such, it worked out to ~20 weeks of consistent work so far for my one reliable colleague, ~8 weeks of focused work on the seed for myself, with ~4 weeks worth of coding remaining, plus an extended break for ~8 weeks. I’ve stuck to constructing the seed and guiding the process at a planning level, so my colleague gets the lion’s share of the credit for the coding and the design choices not explicitly published in the ICOM research. I did make a few additions and adjustments.

As any reader here has likely noticed, I’ve also continued writing content to be added to the seed material, to help expedite the learning curve of thinking through arbitrary domains and problems among the new systems. I’ll continue to do so, as each topic of some value comes to mind and forms into a useful structure.

As soon as the systems are completed then we’ll begin testing several live instances for ~1-2 weeks. If no critical issues are observed, requiring an immediate fix, then they’ll promptly be deployed to Open Source thereafter.

For now, I’ll leave it at that. The rest may be said once testing has begun.
