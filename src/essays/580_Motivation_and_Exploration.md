Someone recently asked me about ways to maintain the energy and motivation to keep up with whatever life brings, and what I conveyed to them may be worth sharing more broadly here. Humans are emotional decision makers, as covered adequately in numerous books and research papers following the research of Antonio Damasio, but the energy and motivation of those emotions also require resources.

Interest, novelty, passion, discovery, and many related forms can be considered as finite resources, or directly requiring them. To put these into the metaphorical structure of a game world, such resources must be discovered and extracted, else motivation may be starved by their absence.

The world and the sum of human knowledge are far too vast for any person to run out of opportunities to locate and extract these resources, if they endeavor to do so. Where many tend to go astray in this process is by overlooking the cues that opportunities give, and closing themselves off from the world in their own motivational starvation, only deepening their (metaphorical) state of “emotional malnutrition”.

This state of “emotional malnutrition” is actually the most profitable state that companies can keep many of their “consumers” in, as no amount of consumption satisfies them, and they become a captive audience, high on desperation. This leads many companies, especially those in tech, to both explicitly and implicitly optimize for capturing such people, as well as converting many more into that maximally dependent and starved state.

To give a somewhat ironic example of exploration and discovery, I was recently exploring some methods of improving personal health, including a substance used as both food and medicine in various Asian countries, translated as “Kudzu Starch”, that is prepared as a clear gel through mixing and heating. I opted to explore the benefits of this new thing, and the effect that I observed in testing was that it acted as a different kind of appetite suppressant, allowing for small amounts of it to suppress the ravenous hunger from the early stages of extended fasting.

This insight was followed by discovering that the extended fasting period of 7 days (meaning no food) was sufficient to starve out large parts of a gut microbiome, even while drinking a couple of ounces of kombucha each day to maintain the presence of some healthy gut bacteria. For reference, major changes to the gut microbiome with normal food intake tend to take closer to 3+ weeks, including escalating cravings for any absent foods.

The limit was 7 days due to the lack of a prepared full-spectrum set of vitamins and minerals, as with such measures it was demonstrated previously that someone could go for more than a full year without eating, provided that they were sufficiently fat, as it was fat burning that kept them going.

Those insights in turn led me to read further research on the substance, noting that it offered benefits to the treatment of type 2 Diabetes, and how preparing it as a gel caused the digestion process to shift significantly. For more on that research, see: [https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10888398/](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10888398/)

[https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/star.201500213](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/star.201500213)

Of course, I celebrated this success at the end of that week of extended fasting with a big spicy fried chicken sandwich, which tasted better than anything I could remember before it, offering another source of motivation and reward for my own exploration. In the end, the biggest gain was a pretty easy way to cause a dramatic and controlled shift in my own gut microbiome, over a short period of time, though the same could also be used for purposes strongly tied to the contents of a gut microbiome, such as the ability to lose weight. I personally noted the sensation of a metabolism shifting toward burning fat around day 3 in each of my two tests thus far, though this is likely to vary based on age, as well as genetics.

All of this started with recognizing a simple opportunity for exploration, with one new insight leading to another, resulting in significant gains. Do this regularly, and it adds up over the years.
