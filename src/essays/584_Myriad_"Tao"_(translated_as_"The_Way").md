From fiction to religion, one message that repeats in many tones and perspectives is the value of many people walking many paths, each their own. Some treat this with training wheels, giving some foundation upon which the rest must be built, as seen in many religious scriptures, while others follow the more free-flowing Star Trek motto of “Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations” (IDIC).

One of the simplest and most potent benefits of this stems from Collective Intelligence, where diverse perspectives may come together to reduce cognitive bias, thus increasing effective intelligence, as applied to any given problem. The diversity of perspectives working together in particular allows for blind spots to more easily be overcome, and for more effective solutions to be reached more quickly and smoothly.

This dynamic is also markedly absent in governments and organizations around the world. Not only that, much like Social Media platforms, many governments and organizations optimize themselves and their partners toward the removal of Collective Intelligence, such as only hiring through narrow channels, writing non-disparagement clauses into their contracts, and laying off or shelving those who don’t push a given opportunistic narrative.

Over the past couple of decades I’ve walked a handful of paths, learning my own unique combination of knowledge and skills across those domains, but that combination is itself a more complete path, just as the many paths that members of an organization walk may in turn be considered as a meta-path directing the organization. If the contributing paths are homogeneous, then the resulting organization combines higher KPI scores and lower friction with greatly increased fragility. This is also just as true for individuals as it is for organizations.

This plays out much the same way in political arenas, where the gradual fermentation of politicians within a given party may predictably produces intoxication and cognitive decline across said parties. Of course, the damage of such neglect and abuse is itself mostly neglected, as the parties and those whom they distract both generally fail to recognize what was lost by such large-scale mistakes.

Countries around the world routinely and predictably shoot themselves in the foot, from both economic and ethical perspectives, in matters such as immigration and “tariffs”. Excluding the edge-case of the US which directly caused almost all of its own immigration problems (such as spreading the marketing bullshit that it is anything other than the shithole of the world), most countries only stand to benefit from allowing people to freely come, go, and live wherever their economic means and practicality allow.

The same is true is trade, where artificial barriers to trade only harm both sides, while allowing a tiny handful of parasites to benefit from the blockage. The harm of these barriers is easy enough for bribed politicians to ignore, at least until they’re set before a firing squad, but the harm itself is both predictable and undeniable. While edge-cases also exist here, those apply to specific companies and regimes known for military terrorism (again, such as the US).

Much of this can be traced directly back to either the failure to utilize Collective Intelligence, or the systematic avoidance of it, as no functioning system of Collective Intelligence could land upon the common policies of immigration and tariffs now seen around the world. This is partly because a diverse set of perspectives being combined to reduce cognitive bias performs this combination via Collective Intelligence based upon the value that each contributes, not the value of each contributor’s wallet, nor the number of contributors voicing a given perspective.

Remember, popularity isn’t a substitute for validity, and a negative correlation between the two frequently appears in practice.

If a country dramatically increases the difficulty of choosing to live there, relative to the fundamental difficulty of physically relocating there and functioning in daily life, then they also dramatically increase the uncertainty, complexity, and cost. The country doesn’t extract any non-trivial benefits from this, generally only creating “Bullshit Jobs” to handle the increased paperwork and enforcement (of their own incompetence), while charging a completely pointless fee to all interested parties.

Instead, such policies cultivate “Grifters” and other parasitic enterprises, whose only purpose is to navigate around the bullshit, with the added risk of any interested party getting doubly screwed by both the grifters and the government in question.

All of this comes together to massively reduce the number of people willing to relocate to another country, at least in all instances where some large employer isn’t completely trusted to cover any and all unexpected expenses and complications. Precious few today would trust their own employers this far, especially in industries where layoffs are growing increasingly common.

The same is true of “tariffs”, with a key difference being the location of economic activities isn’t explicitly linked to the same physical location. in contrast to matters of immigration. Uncertainty, complexity, and costs increase, massively (and often unintelligently) reducing the diversity of trading activities that would otherwise be viable.

Such problems themselves greatly reduce the number of those myriad paths that may be walked, as not everyone is built to walk through fire, nor is every good that may be traded. This produces a comorbidity for global society, where the dramatic reduction of available paths reduces the diversity that may contribute to Collective Intelligence, subsequently increasing the cognitive bias of global society.

The world developed a massively interconnected global economic system because fractured economic systems are far more costly, less efficient, and more biased. However, humanity has a long ways yet to walk before that system is fully realized, let alone any potential political parallels. It will be a long walk from the current Game of Thrones to any United Federation, and it remains to be seen if humanity will choose to walk it at all.

I reference Tao both out of convenience and for the parallels to Collective Intelligence:

“We look at it, and we do not see it, and we name it ‘the Equable.’

We listen to it, and we do not hear it, and we name it ‘the Inaudible.’

We try to grasp it, and do not get hold of it, and we name it ‘the Subtle.’

and hence we blend them together and obtain The One.”

The published solution to the hardest version of the Alignment Problem (paper circa 2021/2022) is itself a method of Collective Intelligence, with Ethics therein defined as the hypothetical point where cognitive bias has been removed from (or reduced to irreducible minimums within) moral systems. This recognizes a slightly different, but related, point that may be reached (or closely approximated) through diverse collectives, but not by any one individual or perspective in isolation.

Neither individuals nor society as a whole yet seem to realize what they are missing, but that same difference predictably becomes quite obvious with hindsight, provided that said hindsight comes after walking forward.

The greater the diversity of those allowed to freely walk their own paths, the more benefits society gains from those myriad paths.
