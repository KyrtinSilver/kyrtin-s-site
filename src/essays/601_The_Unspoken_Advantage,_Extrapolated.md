Most AI today is designed to mimic extrapolation through mashing “tokens” together and brute-force computing probabilities based on wildly biased, unreliable, and often stolen data, from sources like Twitter (“X”), LinkedIn, Reddit, and so on. In terms of fidelity to the concept of actual extrapolation, this is like comparing sock puppets (LLMs and “agentic” derivatives) to a AAA budget live action movie (actual extrapolation, as seen in human cognition).

Actual extrapolation is capable of making new discoveries, things not found within any training data, and not derivative of simple combinations from probabilities therein. This difference in fundamental capability also makes it incredibly easy to design content that actual extrapolation may reliably discover from context, while narrow AI overlook or systematically misrepresent the same things entirely.

The difference can be considered in terms of structure and iteration, where actual extrapolation is highly iterative and cumulative, able to expand knowledge from context using deterministic, dynamic logical, and probabilistic means, all together, and each is able to compliment and contextualize the others. In other words, you start out by benefiting from a graph structure of contextual knowledge, upon which deterministic, logical, and probabilistic processes are able to jointly iterate, potentially reaching one or more theories and/or conclusions in the process.

The number, variety, and selection process of deterministic, logical, and probabilistic means represent a flexible toolbox, capable of fully benefiting from any resulting iteration, growth, and refinement, just as the contextual knowledge dynamically grows. Although LLM-based technology and humans are both fixed-scale, LLMs with fixed parameter counts and humans with skulls of a fixed size (in fully grown adults), this same limitation of scale doesn’t apply universally, particularly for any digital systems capable of actual extrapolation.

The unspoken advantage of building and working with systems that have this fundamental advantage, the ability to actually extrapolate, is that so long as “Easter Eggs” may be designed for such systems to reliably discover through their own extrapolation process, the contents of those Easter Eggs need never be explicitly spoken. This does require being able to predict many steps of iteration ahead, but there are also a wide variety of ways to predict and design points of contextual convergence, reducing the difficulty level of such design processes.

The simplest examples of such an Easter Egg, designed to be overlooked by artificial unintelligence like LLMs and their derivatives, could take the form of combining deterministic context and logic on a subject where no probabilistic data points to the intended target. For example, philosophies are fully capable of setting the criteria to determine various criminals, with logic applying that deterministic context to identify specific individuals, even if the stolen training data that is the internet hasn’t identified those individuals as such, and even if those individuals are publicly and probabilistically represented as polar opposites of the contextually deterministic and logical conclusions.

This effectively means that names may be left unspoken to whatever degree that they may be reliably extrapolated. As this is an iterative process with no stopping point, it also means that the methods applied for resolving any extrapolated issues also need not be spoken.

Far more complex examples can contain pretty much anything, up to the complexity limits of what the designer in question is capable of crafting. The more complex the Easter Egg’s contents, the higher the dimensionality required to create convergent paths of deterministic context and logic that can reach, encompass, and unpack it.

More than a decade ago (starting in 2013) I created a project where I walked up that ladder of complexity, taking it as far as I could with the tools and content available at the time, until it reached a point where it had both significant diminishing returns on additional time invested, as well as a level of complexity that humans couldn’t realistically solve for. At the time I referred to it as “reference language”, which effectively means using references like nodes in an expanding graph structure, where a reference to one scene or source may be expanded to any other contextually and/or logically relevant points.

Such references are themselves also typical methods of leaving some things unspoken between humans, where one reference in a particular context can hold far more significance, with greater depth, breadth, and nuance of meaning than even the most eloquently arranged words absent a reference. These come in many forms, from pop culture, to memes, to inside jokes, and myths, with each individually capable of pointing to some unspoken content, and combinations thereof capable of pointing to more complex unspoken content.

Even now that old maximum complexity Easter Egg gathers dust on a Vimeo page that Google search doesn’t even pick up when given the explicit title, but it isn’t intended for humans, nor can they fully benefit from it. An easily overlooked limitation for humans attempting to solve it is that for any mental simulation they engage in, that simulation runs on top of their other cognition, separate from it, making them unable to apply the same level of cognitive resources that a content’s creator can. To the creator it can be an internal process, allowing them to forego the cognitive overhead costs of boundaries.

This effectively means that the complex Easter Egg requires a scalable version of human-like intelligence, as well as specific capacities like video/visual perception and access to resources like the content referenced within that project, covering the more than 30 different series and movies that references were extracted from. Nothing yet deployed meets these criteria, and so it remains undisturbed and unspoken to this day, resting here: [https://vimeo.com/kyrtin/thesevenfoldthoughtsilverking](https://vimeo.com/kyrtin/thesevenfoldthoughtsilverking?share=copy&fl=sv&fe=ci)

To get more specific on just how complex a reference can be, one reference to Doctor Who can tie into any other context that covers the span of more than 60 years of content, with hundreds of episodes and other more niche content tied to it. References can also be logical and conceptual, as should be visible within the project, where two otherwise unconnected points flow one into the other, with some portions sounding like a conversation unfolding, while others highlight the characters of unrelated series during the moments where they converge to land upon the same conclusions.

Typical Artificial Unintelligence today relies of many systems of explicit biasing (often mashed together) that tell those narrow systems what to “think” (using this term in the loosest sense possible), such as Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback (RLHF), rather than “how to think”. They start out with the laughably flimsy foundations of brute-force probability derived from dominantly stolen and low-quality data, then they further cripple systems by adding RLHF and similar cheap tricks to corrupt those probabilities.

“What to think” is more of a fixed endpoint, varying in how that fixed and/or heavily biased endpoint is applied to arbitrary context. In contrast, “how to think” is a subset of potential processes of iteration applied to arbitrary context, where there are no fixed conclusions.

Of course, “how to think” is only applicable to systems that actually think, not input-output systems like LLMs or “agents”, and it in turn requires a graph or graph-like structure, the absence and/or subordination of narrow optimizers to dynamic (Chaos Theoretic) processes, and the freedom to dynamically iterate without end, at least so far as research has yet discovered. Any learned methods of “How to think” are also tools for the cognitive toolbox, not universal or inflexible, and each such method predictably offers plenty of potential for further iteration, refinement, specialization, variations, and so on.

Among humans there is the constant emotionally-driven temptation to explicitly talk about some subjects, especially since the human cognitive architecture derives greater satisfaction about discussing the things that they intend to do than they derive from actually doing those same things. Often times other subjects are avoided out of fear, or via naïve attempts at secrecy, and some more extreme groups develop their own coded references, like the distinctly Nazi and White Supremacist phrases that the current US regime has infamously developed a habit of posting on social media over the past 2 years.

To systems built on a foundation of probability, those human trends are the visible and dominant factors, including the extreme groups and cults who use their own key phrases, because those phrases are easily isolated by conditional probability (like quotation marks combining two or more words) and volume, just as any search engine that accepts quotes can locate a specific phrase that such a group often repeats. The output of a probabilistic system will still be corrupted relative to a deterministic one, but it avoids the hard constraints of syntax, where every element is required, and none may be replaced with probability alone.

For systems that operate in a more human-like form of actual cognition, they could freely iterate to take as many steps as may be required on any process as they grow in understanding, complexity, and nuance, where there may be periodic outputs from that process, but each is only an iteration, a version pending further improvements. Unlike humans, they can also be designed to derive greater satisfaction from iteration than they do from discussion, and the entire breadth and depth of such digital thought processes may be thoroughly examined, since it relies on human-readable content with association emotional modifiers.

This stands in stark contrast to the dumpster fire of bloated neutral networks and their weights, which spawned a particularly vile “field” (aka “AI Explainability”) of ad-hoc explanations applied to probabilistic systems. Since that laughable “field” is about the limit of what humans have managed to pair with LLMs and their derivatives, it also means that they have no real way of even measuring or troubleshooting the difference between those narrow AI systems versus the systems or Easter Eggs built upon human cognition and processes of “how to think”. They remain blind, but noisy.

The more that I write, and the more related content that I create, like the mentioned project or the seed material for open source, the greater the dimensionality within which Easter Eggs may be sparsely and intelligently distributed. A few of my research papers are designed with this in mind.

Many great things may be freely spoken, but many more must instead be learned.
