One of the more bizarre things to be found at the intersection of Cognitive Bias research and the investment space (particularly in “AI” today) comes from neglecting a simple fact: “A given set of proxies for estimating the odds of a breakthrough can’t meet or exceed the value of the demonstrated breakthrough itself.”

In 2019 Microsoft placed a $1 billion bet in investment on the odds of OpenAI producing some breakthrough later on. At the time, the company had precisely nothing to justify this investment in terms of demonstrated tech. The same was true again for their first spin-off, Anthropic, as well as numerous (if not every) other OpenAI or Anthropic spin-off since then.

All were bets placed on the odds of a breakthrough, with those odds estimated via proxies, such as perceptions of prestige. Whether or not any of those companies can claim breakthroughs today, none of them started out with such breakthroughs before hundreds of millions, or even billions, had been invested in them.

No matter how many, or which, proxies are used to estimate the odds of a technological breakthrough, such as the often-mentioned (and often-abused) term “AGI”, no combination or set of results among those proxies can ever meet or exceed the value of the guaranteed result for that which they estimate. They estimate odds, and the certainty of demonstrated technology always exceeds the probability of reaching the same, no matter what contributes to those odds.

Someone recently thought to argue against the value of an AI company with demonstrated breakthroughs by citing such proxies, but those proxies are less than worthless, simple distractions, when the purpose of estimating the odds of that which already exists leaves them defunct.

Even simple tools like Google’s Gemini “recognized” this when put to the test, stating that demonstrated technology “leapfrogs” over the risk of early investments built on proxies like a pedigree. Those gambles based on proxies intended to estimate the odds of a breakthrough have frequently landed companies at between 1 and 5 billion dollar valuations over the past 2 years or so.

I won’t go so far as to back Genimi’s estimate that such a company should be valued at 50-150 billion “...right out of the gate, even with zero commercial revenue.”, but mostly because companies don’t need that scale of investment prior to producing their first products and generating some revenue. If humans were rational, then maybe, but Cognitive Bias research exists because they aren’t, not even close.

The further irony is that systems built on layers of rules, rationality, and narrow optimization are of course utterly misaligned with humans, as quite adequately covered in neuroscientist Antonio Damasio’s book on his research, “Descartes’ Error”. Even aiming at rule-based systems within a poorly or unbounded space, like the real world, produces absolute absurdities in endless variety as many try, and inevitably fail, to make purely logical systems operate in open-ended spaces.

Systems that are more meaningfully aligned with how humans actually think, form concepts, and make decisions, are a hard requirement that nearly all so-called “Alignment” and “Ethics” research systematically ignored. They are also among the demonstrated breakthroughs that so-called investors ignored just as systematically (in favor of defunct proxies) for the past 4 years and counting.

One way or another, this industry is due for a change before the end of the year, be it proper funding for demonstrated breakthroughs, or scorched earth for everyone.
