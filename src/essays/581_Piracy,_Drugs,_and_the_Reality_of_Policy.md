In contrast, you need look no further than the current state of the music industry to see what more effective methods look like. The piracy of music has massively decreased since the days of Napster, with even the few remaining by 2017 reduced by a further 65% by 2021. The simple reality is that the inconvenience of downloading and storing individual songs (often with inconsistent quality) on a local device in order to play any music is usually far greater than the monetary cost of paying for a streaming service subscription.

The remaining music piracy has come to mirror the streaming services themselves, accounting for a reported 80% of global activity. That means that the bulk of this activity which remains only manages to do so by reducing the level of inconvenience to users. [https://blog.dataprot.net/piracy-statistics](https://blog.dataprot.net/piracy-statistics)

This clearly illustrates how those with disposable income (as in the people with actual money, not imaginary money) will gladly pay for the increased convenience of goods and services. This same basic principle is also clearly observed with food delivery (vastly superior in Southeast Asia), taxis, Amazon’s shopping (in the US), and numerous other industries, where convenience is a key competitive value.

The music streaming sites largely offer the same spread of content, unlike the dumpster fires calling themselves “streaming video platforms”, marking a sharp point of deviation, where the majority of competitive value is lost in the case of video. Even though both music and video streaming services have the same laughably trivial to break DRM “protections”, the music streaming services tend to offer vastly superior content and convenience compared to their video counterparts.

This illustrates that one of the best ways to actually reduce piracy is simply to treat it like a competitor, making sure that you offer greater convenience, as well as more and higher quality content. For those without the money to pay for services, piracy will remain, as they can’t exchange money for convenience and selection, but they were never going to be paying customers anyway.

If a company and their services can’t offer more and higher quality content and convenience than piracy, they clearly shouldn’t exist.
