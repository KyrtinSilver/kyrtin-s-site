Within any iterative normal process where continuous learning is present, such as the everyday routines and habits of many humans, a process of maturation gradually unfolds. In matters of exploration, where rewards tend to be more sparse and unpredictable in nature, this process of maturation is often accelerated as possibilities are pruned from decision trees.

For example, someone who makes a cup of coffee every morning for a decade or two, slowly exploring many of the possible ingredients, methods, brands, hardware, and so on that may be put to such purposes, as well as the optimal timing within their own routines for doing so, has successfully matured their process. This means that they’ve tested and now better understand all of the things that don’t work, only work under certain conditions, and so on, greatly narrowing the scope of possible actions, timings, and sequences that they take within this process.

The classical quote that “The master has failed more times than the student has tried” points to this dynamic of how expertise and mastery of mature processes develop, with the exploration of many attempts and possibilities, finally resulting in the handful whose intelligent combination may be deemed “mastery”. This can be considered as mitigating “Choice Overload” through the accumulated contextual knowledge covering those choices.

With regard to ICOM-based systems, I refer to the “maturity” of graph nodes and subsets, where that maturity may be measured as the completeness of a connectome for some target or set of nodes, as well as the breadth and depth of content within those nodes. In many cases where the context is unbounded or loosely bounded, the completeness may instead be approximated by the prior learning curve that has progressed over the selected content. As the connectome matures, the optimality of selecting the most relevant and useful context rapidly becomes more focused and intelligent.

In terms of the Explore versus Exploit trade-off (aka the “Multi-Armed Bandit Problem”), the maturation process can also be viewed as the transition from a dominantly exploratory process, to one where the number and/or net potential of opportunities for “exploitation” (as defined within the problem) exceed the number of opportunities offering meaningful potential for exploration. In humans, these learned points of maturation are often entrenched within habits, routines, culture, and ideology, which gradually accumulate, combine, and typically harden as people age.

People tend to grow more “set in their ways” as they age, which also applies to how they think, and what they think, not just what they do and how they do it. This is frequently a net-positive, where older people become happier and far more stable than their younger selves.

However, this is only typical, and exceptions are quite easy to spot. Just as maturation can occur, the reverse is also true, and this reversal can occur through mechanisms of damage, as well as more intentional reversal.

Some research related to this topic has focused on strong correlations between problems like drug abuse and romantic infidelity, where one problem is frequently co-morbid to another, either leading to or worsening the risks and/or realities of the correlated issues. Individuals who fully engage and sustain one or more addictions disrupt the maturation process across the many parts of their lives that those addictions influence.

The industries that heavily rely upon and attempt to optimize for addiction, like the “attention economy”, are inherently invested in preventing the maturation of humans, as such a process reduces the sums of money that they can extract by parasitic means. However, artificially sustaining that state of immaturity across large populations for extended periods and business purposes, especially within echo chambers, is a potent recipe for rapid degeneration along many metrics, resulting in measurable cognitive decline as well as much higher risks of coping mechanism abuse, such as the many forms of substance abuse.

From the perspective of someone who celebrated his 40th birthday this year, I can personally say that the process of maturation partly operates by “hollowing out” the appeal of various “opportunities”, where those opportunities are related sets of possibilities, and more of those possibilities are better informed, more contextually known, as they mature, leaving the “opportunity (sets)” hollowed out proportionate to the members of those sets whose net possibility is known to be unappealing. In other words, higher certainty of shit results for an “opportunity” makes it ring hollow, as opposed to a naïve assessment from a less mature mind.

Take the common example of humanity’s biological drive to reproduce, where a young adult in their 20s is statistically far more inclined toward “exploration”, while someone twice that age tends to have matured a solid grasp of their own preferences. The 20-something adult sees opportunities and potential, leading their perception to lean more on optimism versus pessimism (and/or transient influences like alcohol) as a deciding factor, rather than experience and contextual knowledge. The 40-something adult sees many hollow opportunities, along with a handful of more genuine options, which they in turn appreciate far more than their younger counterparts, better knowing the rarity of that value.

These principles also apply all of the way up society’s many constructs, such as various hierarchies. The more people fall prey to addictions and related comorbities, the less mature and cognitively capable they’ll tend to be. The more mature they already are, the more they’ll tend to resist such hollow opportunities.

While this may correlate with age, that correlation is only as useful as it is specific and relevant, meaning that a broad age-based statistic has virtually no value in the estimation of a typical politician’s cognitive maturity. This is partly because of the many gating and biasing steps that must be taken for anyone to actually become a politician, meaning that only statistics tied specifically to the subset of people who passed those steps may prove more substantively useful.

In contrast to the broader correlation of maturity and aging, add drugs and other addictions to degrade maturation and cognitive capacities, and systematically maximize that degradation across a society, to create something like the current US, or the US tech industry. Just as marketers were found to be more vulnerable to their own BS “marketing” in the research literature, Silicon Valley is full of sniveling addicts nesting within Dark Patterns of their own creation.

The wisdom of society and humanity could certainly be better preserved, integrated, and put to better uses, but for now the words that Mark Twain first spoke in jest, then decades later with poetic irony, come to mind:

“Too bad that youth is wasted on the young.” – Mark Twain (as well as George Bernard Shaw)

To depart from correlation, one could instead say “Too bad (some domain) is wasted on the immature.”
