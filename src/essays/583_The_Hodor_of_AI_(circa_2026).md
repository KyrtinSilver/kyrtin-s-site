The narrative of a story is never fully told. Data is virtually always both incomplete and biased in multiple ways.

Even the fictional stories told in the modern “multi-plot” style of storytelling, such as Game of Thrones, where the story of a fictional world is told from many different points of view at once, are both incomplete and visibly biased by orders of magnitude. Much like these fictional worlds, the real world is also constantly changing, undergoing iteration from countless angles simultaneously.

This effectively means that the narrative is potentially useful, but also virtually always wrong, as the data is incomplete, biased, and constantly changing. If computers operated under such conditions they couldn’t even complete a start-up sequence, like if your own narrative was that of “Hodor” from Game of Thrones, waiting for that one day where everything finally lined up and no syntax errors were thrown. In contrast, life adapts, persists, and some of it thrives (population dynamics).

The process of “life” and growth may itself be measured in how each individual instance of that life manages these factors. With incomplete, biased, and constantly changing data there is a heavy selection pressure to encourage countless and ever-changing forms of adaptation, among which many entirely different adaptations may be equally viable in a general sense, and deeply specialized in others.

The superficial study of incomplete data, naively aggregated, and subjected to the narrow optimization of statistics, produces gross misrepresentations of reality, such as the infamous case of “targeted advertising”, which in studies was shown to be no more effective in actuality than the older form of the same.

The actual effort required to develop non-trivial understanding of a given person, let alone a population, is considerable. It also remains out of reach to those who rely on the crutch of typical “AI” such as LLMs and their derivative “agents”, which are designed to obstruct and degrade such a process.

This necessitates the creation of a hierarchy of effort as an interim step of learning, for practical purposes, where some subset of possible targets of study are selected, and the effort to study them is increased by a single step up the ladder. This process may repeat several times, as targets requiring greater study, due to rarity, time-sensitive context, opportunity, or other such purposes, are respectively discovered.

The dimensionality of such a possibility space itself necessitates a human-like motivational system for purposes of effective exploration, as no combination of narrow optimizers, “reinforcement learning” policies, and/or router systems (such as MoE), are capable of handling such a space effectively. As demonstrated in the 7th gen ICOM-based research system of years previous, a seed of data less than 1 GB in size can accomplish this, and that seed may grow under arbitrary conditions as it constantly learns and adapts.

Even the simplest seeds also offer immense value, both to human researchers and other ICOM-based systems, as unlike their human counterparts they are vastly more transparent, their data and structure easily and cleanly transferable and adjustable. One of the biggest limitations in the study of the human brain is a matter of lacking the tools necessary to monitor activity with high fidelity, let alone to truly unpack what that activity encompasses or adjusting how the system engaged in it operates.

These problems don’t exist for ICOM-based systems, and even if the results of studying such systems only produce new theories for how the human brain may work, those theories can be a great deal better informed, raising their subsequent accuracy and speed of iteration.

Hopefully the ending for our current season of Silicon Valley’s Tech Industry will be similar to that of Game of Thrones, with a significant reduction in the number of surviving main characters.
